<h3 class="sec">The problem of block 6</h3>

<p>A store that holds up but changes without a net faces three threats: the faulty version, Black Friday 2027 and the 23:00 report. Each piece below answers one, and the last one ties the previous ones together.</p>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Change with a small radius (module 22).</strong> <code>pedidos</code> goes from 2.4.0 to 2.5.0, which returns <code>201</code> with <strong>shipping 0.00</strong> in Recife: healthy, no error, wrong. <em>Recreate</em> takes everything down for <strong>40 s</strong>, <em>rolling</em> takes <strong>90 s</strong>, <em>blue/green</em> switches all at once and <em>canary</em> goes from 10 to 50 to 100%. The radius is fraction × time × cost: <strong>R$ 54,000</strong> with <em>rolling</em> against <strong>R$ 450</strong> with a 10% <em>canary</em>. <em>Shadow</em>, the <code>frete_v2</code> <em>flag</em> and rings per <em>shard</em> complete the set. The order is <strong>1059</strong>. <a href={L('deployment')}>Deployment Models</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Plan for the peak (23).</strong> Changing well does not say whether the store survives <strong>Black Friday on November 26, 2027</strong>. Little's Law (L = λ × W) and the <strong>70%</strong> target ρ (near the knee the wait explodes) give the plan for <code>catalogo</code>, <code>pedidos</code>, <code>pagamentos</code> and <code>estoque</code>: <strong>14 · 10 · 15 · 2 = 41 vCPU</strong>, for 950 req/s and 200 orders/s. <a href={L('capacity-planning')}>Capacity Planning</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Test the plan (24).</strong> A plan without a test is a hypothesis. k6 shows that the store "today" breaks at <strong>36/s</strong> and the "plan" reaches <strong>~250/s</strong>, with goals of p95 ≤ <strong>800 ms</strong> and errors ≤ <strong>0.1%</strong>. The mixed scenario finds what the math did not: the <strong>23:00 report</strong> fighting for the pool. <a href={L('testes-de-carga')}>Load and Stress Testing</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Split what is shared (25).</strong> The test's finding has a remedy: the <strong>20-connection</strong> pool becomes <strong>16 + 4</strong>. Without the split, the report leaves <strong>33%</strong> of checkout and <strong>10%</strong> of status with <code>503</code>. With it, the hot shard's radius drops from <strong>60% to 40%</strong>. The <em>lanes</em> are <strong>12 · 6 · 2</strong> and <code>mkt-01</code> has a ceiling of 100 req/s on the catalog. <a href={L('bulkhead')}>Bulkhead Pattern</a>.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">Take a piece out: the mixed test</h3>

<p>Without the mixed load test, the 23:00 report only shows up in production, with <strong>33%</strong> of checkout and <strong>10%</strong> of status on <code>503</code>. Without the <em>canary</em> and the business metric, shipping 0.00 lasts 60 min and costs <strong>R$ 54,000</strong> (or <strong>R$ 486,000</strong>, if only the 23:00 report finds it). Modules 22, 24 and 25 warned about it.</p>

<h3 class="sec">Can you do it?</h3>

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>Say the radius of a deploy (fraction × time × cost).</li>
  <li>Calculate replicas with Little and the target ρ.</li>
  <li>Design a load test and say what it does <strong>not</strong> prove.</li>
  <li>Split a pool and say what the batch loses.</li>
  <li>Say why the 1 ÷ N table misleads.</li>
</ol>
