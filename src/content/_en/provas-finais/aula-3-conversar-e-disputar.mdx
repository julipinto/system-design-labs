<h3 class="sec">The problem of block 3</h3>

<p>Seven services that call each other and touch the same row raise three questions, one for each piece: who gets the cat, how much it costs to wait for the other side, and what to do with the event that needs no response.</p>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Compete (module 11).</strong> Bia and Caio want the <strong>last cat</strong> at the same time: with no protection, stock goes to <strong>−1</strong>. A <em>mutex</em> in the process is not enough with 2 replicas of <code>estoque</code> (that is 2 memories), so a <strong>distributed lock</strong> on Redis comes in. Whoever gets it first wins (Bia, <strong>1049</strong>) and the other receives <code>409</code>. The math: 2 ms of CPU + 18 ms of waiting gives 50 orders/s at a time and a ceiling of <strong>500/s</strong>. The study also covers <em>deadlock</em> and <em>starvation</em>. <a href={L('concorrencia')}>Concurrency and Parallelism</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Wait (12).</strong> With the lock solved, what is left is the cost of each call. REST with JSON × gRPC with Protobuf: 1050 in Protobuf is <strong>11 bytes</strong> (without the gRPC header), and the PSP answers in <strong>210 ms</strong>. When the PSP says Davi's Pix went through without the store asking, that is the <strong>webhook</strong> for <strong>1050</strong>; <em>short</em> and <em>long polling</em> ask, the WebSocket keeps the connection open, and GraphQL lets the client choose the fields. <a href={L('comunicacao-sincrona')}>Synchronous Communication</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Do not wait (13).</strong> Not everything needs a response right away. In <strong>Kafka</strong>, the <code>pedidos</code> topic has 3 partitions and the <code>pedido_id</code> key picks the partition (1049 lands on <code>p0</code>). Each order generates <strong>4 events</strong>; on Black Friday that is <strong>120/s</strong> on the topic and <strong>40/s per partition</strong>. The <code>estoque</code>, <code>entregas</code> and <code>notificacoes</code> groups consume, and the <strong>DLQ</strong> holds the message that cannot be processed. MQTT talks to the truck and AMQP to the emails. <a href={L('comunicacao-assincrona')}>Asynchronous Communication: Messaging, Events and Streaming</a>.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">Take a piece out: the distributed lock</h3>

<p>Without the distributed lock, two replicas of <code>estoque</code> sell the last cat twice and stock ends at <strong>−1</strong>. Module 11 warned about it.</p>
