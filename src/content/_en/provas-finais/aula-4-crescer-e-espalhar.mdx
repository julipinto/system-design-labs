<h3 class="sec">The problem of block 4</h3>

<p>At 20:03 the store is still standing and yet it fails for some customers. The average dashboard says everything is fine. The four pieces below answer, in order: what to measure, which direction to grow, how to split the data and how to copy it.</p>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Measure (module 14).</strong> The average is <strong>135 ms</strong> and the p50 is <strong>32 ms</strong>, but the p95 is <strong>800 ms</strong> and the p99 is <strong>2,000 ms</strong>. <code>catalogo-3</code> is at 99% and the whole fleet reads <strong>67.5%</strong>: (9 × 64 + 99) ÷ 10. The weakest link rules: <code>pagamentos</code> does <strong>40/s</strong> and the <code>pedidos</code> database, <strong>150/s</strong>. Davi's order is <strong>1052</strong>. <a href={L('performance')}>Performance, Capacity and Scalability</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Choose the axis (15).</strong> With the bottleneck measured, what is left is how to grow. On the cube, <strong>X</strong> clones (replicas), <strong>Y</strong> splits by function (services) and <strong>Z</strong> splits by data (customers). The store goes from <strong>(10, 9, 1)</strong> to <strong>(10, 9, 3)</strong>, and the output rises from <strong>90 to 150 to 200</strong> orders per second. Caio's order is <strong>1053</strong>. <a href={L('scale-cube')}>Scale Cube</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Split by a key (16).</strong> The Z axis needs a key. That is <strong>3 <em>shards</em></strong> by <code>cliente_id</code> with <code>murmur3</code> over <strong>50 million</strong> rows. <code>mkt-01</code> has 40% of the rows and leaves <code>shard-2</code> with 60%, and efficiency drops to <strong>55.6%</strong>. Going from 3 to 4 shards moves <strong>75.2%</strong> of the data with <code>mod</code> and <strong>25.7%</strong> with the ring. The <em>shuffle</em> forms <strong>45</strong> pairs. Lena's 1054 lands on <code>shard-1</code>. <a href={L('sharding')}>Sharding and Partitioning</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Copy (17).</strong> Splitting does not protect against a burned disk: each shard needs a replica. With the primary in SP and replicas in Rio and Recife, the commit costs <strong>2 ms</strong> (asynchronous), <strong>12 ms</strong> (semi) or <strong>82 ms</strong> (synchronous). The lag is <strong>300 ms</strong> in Rio and <strong>800 ms</strong> in Recife; the RPO is <strong>9 to 24</strong> orders and the RTO, <strong>35 s</strong>. Lena does not see <strong>1055</strong>: she reads the Recife replica, 800 ms behind. <a href={L('replicacao')}>Data Replication</a>.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">Take a piece out: the replicas</h3>

<p>Without replicas, <code>shard-1</code> goes down and Lena and Bia <strong>are left with no orders</strong>, and a burned disk takes the data. Modules 16 and 17 warned about it.</p>
