<h3 class="sec">The problem of block 1</h3>

<p>A one-server store is a program and a disk. It works until the first incident of each kind: the data travels and takes time, the disk burns, the databases disagree and the plush cat page gets slow. That is five problems, and each piece below came in to solve one of them.</p>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The data arrives (module 1).</strong> Lena opens the app in Recife: DNS finds <code>loja.com.br</code>, TCP opens the connection, TLS encrypts and HTTP asks for the page. The 313 KB photo becomes <strong>215 slices</strong> of up to 1,460 B, and each round trip to SP costs <strong>80 ms</strong>. <a href={L('protocolos-de-rede')}>Network Protocols</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>The data stays (2).</strong> With the data arriving, it has to survive a burned disk. <code>pedidos.db</code> goes onto a <strong>RAID 10</strong>, whose small write costs <strong>2 I/Os</strong>, and the photos go to <em>object storage</em>. On RAID 0 the write costs 1 I/O and the first disk that burns takes everything. <a href={L('storage-raid-io')}>Storage, RAID and I/O</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>The databases agree (3).</strong> When the data lives in three sites (SP, Rio and Recife), they can lose the network between them. CAP forces a choice, <strong>during the partition</strong>, between refusing (consistency) and accepting (availability). The majority is the <strong>2-of-3 quorum</strong>, and the synchronous write to Recife takes ~80 ms. <a href={L('cap-acid-base')}>CAP Theorem, ACID and BASE</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>The data is found (4).</strong> With the sites in agreement, what is left is finding one row: the <code>pedidos</code> table holds <strong>1042</strong> (Lena, cat, 89.90). The <strong>B-Tree index</strong> finds the row in a few page reads. At 1 million rows it beats the scan; at 6 rows it ties, because everything fits in one page. <a href={L('databases')}>Databases, Data Models and Indexing</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>The page is fast (5).</strong> Finding fast is still not enough for the page everyone opens. <strong>Redis</strong> keeps the response, and with a 90% hit rate the average time is 0.9 × 1 + 0.1 × 41 = <strong>5 ms</strong>. The <strong>CDN</strong> in Recife brings the photo closer, and the promotion drops the price from <strong>89.90 to 79.90</strong>. <a href={L('cache')}>Cache Strategies</a>.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">Take a piece out: the cache</h3>

<p>Without the cache, the average time goes from <strong>5 ms</strong> to <strong>41 ms</strong>, 8.2 times more, and the catalog at 640 req/s sends everything to the origin instead of the 128 req/s that arrive with an 80% hit rate. Modules 5 and 21 warned about it.</p>
