<h3 class="sec">The problem of block 2</h3>

<p>The single program has a factory defect: everything shares CPU, memory and fate. A heavy report and Lena's order fight for the same machine. The five pieces below come in the order in which the cut creates a new need.</p>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Cut (module 6).</strong> The program becomes <strong>7 services</strong>: <code>catalogo</code>, <code>carrinho</code>, <code>pedidos</code>, <code>pagamentos</code>, <code>estoque</code>, <code>entregas</code> and <code>relatorios</code>, each with <strong>its own database</strong>. The report goes down alone. The price is the network: the 180 ms checkout becomes <strong>200 ms</strong>, with 4 hops of 5 ms. On Black Friday, instead of 10 copies of the monolith (<strong>80 vCPU</strong>), only the heavy services scale (<strong>25 vCPU</strong>). Lena's order is <strong>1047</strong>, with a <code>trace_id</code>. <a href={L('microsservicos')}>Microservices, Monoliths and Domains</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Distribute (7).</strong> With several services and several replicas, someone has to pick the replica. <code>lb-loja</code> (203.0.113.10) uses algorithms, and a round robin that is "fair" in number of orders is not fair in work: 3 searches of 900 ms can land on the same replica and add up to <strong>2.7 s</strong>. A 5 s <em>healthcheck</em> with 3 failures takes the replica out in <strong>15 s</strong>. <a href={L('load-balancers')}>Load Balancers and Reverse Proxies</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>One door (8).</strong> Behind the balancer the app still knows every service. <code>gw-loja</code> brings them behind one door: it validates the <strong>JWT</strong> (no token, <code>401</code>; token without the scope, <code>403</code>), applies <em>rate limiting</em>, separates <code>/v1</code> from <code>/v2</code> and forwards <code>X-Cliente-Id: lena-77</code>. The order is <strong>1048</strong>. <a href={L('api-gateways')}>API Gateways</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>One screen (9).</strong> The door is single, but the app's home screen still makes 4 calls of 120 ms: <strong>480 ms and 180 KB</strong>. <code>bff-app</code> makes the calls in parallel and delivers <strong>150 ms and 12 KB</strong>. If <code>recomendacoes</code> goes down, it waits 100 ms and responds without the section (<code>"recomendado": []</code>). <a href={L('bff')}>Backend for Frontends (BFF)</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>One standard (10).</strong> With everything connected, each service repeats retry, timeout and mTLS its own way. The <strong>mesh</strong> takes that out of the code: one <em>sidecar</em> per pod (30 sidecars add up to <strong>3 vCPU and 1.5 GB</strong>), mTLS and retry with a rule. Without a rule, 3 layers of 3 attempts make <strong>27</strong> calls to <code>pagamentos</code> per click. <a href={L('service-mesh')}>Service Mesh</a>.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">Take a piece out: the gateway</h3>

<p>Without the gateway, the app knows <strong>6 addresses</strong>, each service does its own login and <em>rate limiting</em>, and the 500 req/s bot reaches the service directly. Module 8 warned about it.</p>
