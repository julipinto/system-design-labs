<p>In the last lesson we were left with three teams and a promise: to map a real purchase. Instead of starting from the tables, the case starts from what happens. Lena sees the cat, puts it in the cart, pays, and the package arrives. Each step is a fact the business recognizes, and it is around these facts that the contexts, entities and aggregates appear.</p>

<OneLiner>Map the events of a purchase in order first, group them by who owns each one, and only then look for the entities, value objects and aggregates.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The events, in order.</strong> Lena's purchase is <code>ProdutoVisto</code> → <code>ItemAdicionadoAoCarrinho</code> → <code>PedidoCriado</code> → <code>PedidoPago</code> → <code>EstoqueReservado</code> → <code>PedidoEnviado</code> → <code>PedidoEntregue</code>. All in the past tense, in the language of the business. The technique is called <strong>event storming</strong> (Alberto Brandolini): get the people who know the business together and put the events on a wall, in order.</li>
  <li><strong>Group into contexts.</strong> Ask who owns each fact: <code>ProdutoVisto</code> belongs to Catalog, <code>ItemAdicionadoAoCarrinho</code> to Cart, <code>PedidoCriado</code> to Orders, <code>PedidoPago</code> to Payments, <code>EstoqueReservado</code> to Stock. <code>PedidoEnviado</code> and <code>PedidoEntregue</code> stay with Shipping. Where a word changes meaning, a boundary runs through.</li>
  <li><strong>Find the entities.</strong> Look for what has identity and lives through the events: the <strong>Order</strong> (1047), the <strong>Customer</strong> (Lena), the <strong>Product</strong> (42) and the <strong>Parcel</strong> (<code>Volume</code>) that leaves through the warehouse door.</li>
  <li><strong>Find the value objects.</strong> Lena's <strong>Address</strong> and <code>Dinheiro(94.90, "BRL")</code>: they are worth their values and are swapped whole.</li>
  <li><strong>Find the aggregate.</strong> The <code>Pedido</code> is the root, with <code>ItemDoPedido</code> inside, and the invariant <em>total = 79.90 + 15.00 = 94.90</em>. The Customer and the Product are other aggregates: the order only keeps their number, not the whole object.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li>Events first avoid the classic mistake of modeling from the tables. The verbs of the business show who does what, and the tables come after.</li>
  <li>The boundary shows up where two contexts use the same word differently, or where an event leaves one and enters the other. <code>PedidoPago</code> leaves Payments and enters Stock.</li>
  <li>A small aggregate is better than a big one. Referring to the Product by number, and not by its insides, keeps transactions small and contexts independent.</li>
  <li>The map already points to the services: each context with an owner is a candidate. Next: how those owners talk without reading each other's database.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={4}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">What an event storming looks like</h4>
  <p>In Brandolini's version, the wall has orange sticky notes for events, in order, left to right. Then come the commands (what someone asks for), the actors and, finally, the groupings that become aggregates and contexts. The whole map comes out of a conversation of a few hours, with questions like "what happens next?" and "who needs to know this?".</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Entity or value object?</h4>
  <p>Test question: if the two are swapped for identical copies, would anyone notice? If Lena has two delivery addresses with the same fields, it does not matter which is which: value object. If there are two orders with the same items, the difference matters (one was already paid, the other was not): entity.</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">On the size of the aggregate</h4>
  <p>Each aggregate is usually saved in a single transaction. If the <code>Pedido</code> included the whole <code>Produto</code>, every price change in Catalog would touch the orders. That is why the order keeps <code>produto: 42</code> and the price at the time of purchase (79.90), not a live reference.</p>
