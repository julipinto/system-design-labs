<p class="ponte">Last lesson the store copied data by reading the log, and all of that assumed <strong>one</strong> owner per piece of data. But in lesson 2 two primaries wrote the same address, and <em>last write wins</em> threw away the right write because of a clock 120 ms behind. What is missing is a way for two sites to write at the same time and for the copies to <strong>merge without conflict</strong>, with nobody deciding who wins.</p>

<OneLiner>A <strong>CRDT</strong> is a data structure whose copies <strong>can always be merged</strong> without conflict, because the merge is <strong>commutative, associative and idempotent</strong>: order and repetitions do not change the result. It guarantees that the copies <strong>converge</strong>, not that the result <strong>makes sense for the business</strong>.</OneLiner>

Then press <strong>Limit</strong>: both sites sell the last mug, and the merge gives <strong>−1</strong>.</p>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>G-Counter</strong> (only grows). The <code>visualizacoes</code> of cat 42 keep <strong>one counter per site</strong>: <code>{"{SP: 120, Rio: 80, Recife: 50}"}</code>, total <strong>250</strong>. In the partition between SP and Recife, SP counts +15 (<code>SP: 135</code>, total 265) and Recife counts +8 (<code>Recife: 58</code>, total 258). The <strong>merge is the maximum per site</strong>: <code>{"{SP: 135, Rio: 80, Recife: 58}"}</code>, total <strong>273</strong>, the same in both directions and the same if applied twice. With <em>last write wins</em> on the total, it would be 265 or 258, and the store would lose 8 or 15 views.</li>
  <li><strong>OR-Set</strong> (set, <em>observed-remove</em>). Lena's cart starts with <code>{"{caneca}"}</code>. In the partition, the <strong>phone</strong> (Recife) adds the <code>gato</code> and the <strong>laptop</strong> (SP) removes the <code>caneca</code>. The merge gives <code>{"{gato}"}</code>: both intentions count. If one side <strong>removes</strong> the mug and the other <strong>adds it again</strong> at the same time, the mug <strong>stays</strong>: "add wins", because each addition has its own tag and the removal only deletes the tags it saw.</li>
  <li><strong>LWW-Register</strong> (the latest timestamp wins). It is the address case from lesson 2: SP's write (timestamp 20:20:00.100) beats Recife's (timestamp .030, clock 120 ms behind) even though Recife's is the most recent in reality. LWW is a simple CRDT and <strong>depends on a clock</strong>. The <strong>version vector</strong> (<code>{"{SP:2, Rec:0}"}</code> against <code>{"{SP:1, Rec:1}"}</code>) does not choose: it <strong>detects</strong> that the writes are concurrent and hands the decision back to the application.</li>
  <li><strong>PN-Counter</strong> (goes up and down): two G-Counters, one for increments and one for decrements, and the value is the difference. The mug's <strong>stock</strong> has 1 unit. In the partition, SP sells 1 and Recife sells 1, and each sees <strong>0</strong>. The merge gives <strong>−1</strong>: the CRDT <strong>converged</strong> (the two sites agree) and <strong>violated</strong> the rule "stock ≥ 0".</li>
  <li><strong>Convergence.</strong> Merging the same set of states in any order, with duplicate messages, gives the same result: the test with 5 messages in <strong>120 permutations</strong> (5!) gave a single final state. It is the commutativity, associativity and idempotence from the article, and in Shapiro's paper the merge is the <em>join</em> of a semilattice.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">State or operations, where they live and the cost</h3>

<p>There are two families (Shapiro): <strong>CvRDTs</strong>, which exchange the <strong>state</strong> and merge it, as in the examples above, and <strong>CmRDTs</strong>, which exchange the <strong>operations</strong> and require causal delivery. CRDTs live in collaborative editors (the article's example; <a href="https://github.com/automerge/automerge">Automerge</a> and <a href="https://yjs.dev/">Yjs</a>), in Redis Active-Active and in Riak. The cost is <strong>metadata that grows</strong>: tags and <em>tombstones</em> (removal marks) cannot be discarded at will, the subject of "CRDTs: The Hard Parts" by Kleppmann.</p>

<h3 class="sec">If it goes wrong: the last item sold twice</h3>

<p>The stock PN-Counter <strong>sells the last item twice</strong>. Both sites accepted the sale because each saw 1 in stock, and the merge, correct and equal on both sides, shows <strong>−1</strong>. Convergence does not protect an invariant. For the last item, module 11 still applies (the decrement with <code>UPDATE … AND quantidade &gt; 0</code> and a <strong>single owner</strong> of the stock) or module 19 (compensating the extra sale with a refund).</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The merge is a calculation, not a choice.</strong> 135 + 80 + 58 = 273, with nobody deciding, and without losing Recife's 8 views.</li>
  <li><strong>Each type solves one case.</strong> Counter for views, set for the cart, register with a version for the address.</li>
  <li><strong>LWW depends on a clock.</strong> The version vector at least warns that there was concurrency.</li>
  <li><strong>Converging is not being right.</strong> The stock at −1 is the proof: a business rule needs an owner or a compensation.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

  <h4 class="deep-h">Correction to the source article: what CRDTs guarantee</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>"Guarantee that conflicts are resolved automatically".</strong> They guarantee <strong>convergence</strong>, not that the result makes sense for the business (the stock at −1). The example of <strong>editing the same line of a text</strong> only works with CRDTs designed for text (sequences), not with an LWW. The right properties: the merge is commutative, associative and idempotent. The article says "associative, commutative and idempotent operations", which holds for state; CmRDT operations have other requirements, such as causal delivery.</li>
  </ul>

<p class="gancho">The store now copies data in every way: between databases, between services, by reading the log and by merging concurrent writes. And Lena's "my orders" screen still reads from the <strong>same table</strong> the checkout writes to. What is left is what changes if she reads from a <strong>copy built only for reading</strong>, fed by CDC: how to separate the write model from the read model. That is module 18, CQRS.</p>
