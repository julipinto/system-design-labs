<p class="ponte">Last lesson <code>PedidoEnviado</code> left the Store, went through the bus and reached whoever listens. What is left is what that listener does with it. Lena opens the tracking screen and wants to see <code>enviado</code>, <strong>now</strong>, without the system having to re-read the events of 1047 on every tap. The Store was built to <strong>write</strong> at volume; for reading, the store builds something else. That something is the <strong>projection</strong>.</p>

<OneLiner>The <strong>projection</strong> listens to the events and maintains a <strong>read model</strong>: the CQRS of module 18, with the Event Store in place of the write database. It is a <strong>pure function</strong> of the events, and that is why the model is <strong>disposable</strong>: it can be rebuilt.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The Store is not for querying.</strong> It is optimized for writing events at volume, and only keeps "what happened". For "how is 1047 doing?" you would have to fold its events. For "which orders are <code>enviado</code>?" you would have to fold those of <strong>all</strong> orders.</li>
  <li><strong>The projection reads the events and writes the model.</strong> For each event that arrives, it applies a small rule: <code>PedidoEnviado</code> sets <code>status = enviado</code> and <code>rastreio = LA123456789BR</code> on 1047's row. The result is the <strong>read model</strong>, in the shape of the screen.</li>
  <li><strong>Several models, one Store.</strong> In the store: <code>meus_pedidos</code> (module 18), the <strong>status of 1047</strong> (the tracking screen) and revenue by city. Each one has its own projection and its own database, and all of them read the <strong>same</strong> events.</li>
  <li><strong>Same sequence, same result.</strong> The projection is a <strong>pure function</strong> of the events: it does not look at the clock (<code>now()</code>) or call an external service. The same 4 events of 1047, on any machine at any time, give the same model.</li>
  <li><strong>That is why it is disposable.</strong> If the model gets corrupted, you delete it and <strong>reprocess</strong> from the start. The Store is the truth; the model is only the copy, and the copy can be remade.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">Three ways to maintain the model</h3>

<p>The article describes three, which differ in <strong>when</strong> the model is up to date and in <strong>who pays</strong> for it:</p>

<div class="sr-tablewrap">
<table class="sr-table">
  <thead><tr><th>Way</th><th>How it works</th><th>What it gives</th><th>What it costs</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><th>Transactional</th><td>the projection is written <strong>in the same transaction</strong> as the event, in the same database</td><td>reads always up to date, no lag</td><td>weighs on the Store and becomes a bottleneck at volume</td></tr>
    <tr><th>Semi-synchronous</th><td>the Store commits and publishes; the projection updates right after, in another process</td><td>the Store keeps only the write: the "source of truth", and the model is the "safe derived state"</td><td>a lag of a few milliseconds, and only works where waiting is acceptable</td></tr>
    <tr><th>Asynchronous</th><td>the events travel over the bus and each domain builds its own model</td><td>decoupled from the Store; each team picks its own database</td><td>eventual consistency, and each rebuild requires sending the events in full</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
</div>

<p>The author's lab has both sides. According to the README, the <code>ledger</code> updates <strong>transactional</strong> models (<code>accounts</code> and <code>transactions</code>) inside the <strong>same SQL transaction</strong> as the event and only then publishes to Kafka. The balance (ScyllaDB) and the statement (MongoDB) are <strong>asynchronous</strong> models, built by consumers. It was read, not executed.</p>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the projection with a bug</h3>

<p>A <em>deploy</em> of the status projection starts writing <code>enviado</code> for <code>PedidoEntregue</code>. For two hours the screen shows <code>enviado</code> for orders already delivered. Fixing the code does not fix the <strong>rows already written</strong>. But the events are intact in the Store. The fix is to <strong>delete the model and reprocess from the start</strong>.</p>

<p>The math: 200 million events (50 million orders × 4) at 20,000 per second (the module 18 rate) is 10,000 s, or <strong>2 h 47 min</strong>. Reading the Store is <strong>not</strong> the bottleneck: measured on PostgreSQL 14, it returns ~555 thousand events per second, so reading the 200 million would take about 6 minutes. What takes time is <strong>writing</strong> to the model. Without the Event Store, with only the Kafka topic (7 days), there would be <strong>nowhere</strong> to rebuild from.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The Store writes and the model answers.</strong> The screen reads a ready-made row, in its own shape, instead of folding events.</li>
  <li><strong>The projection is a pure function.</strong> Same sequence of events, same model; no <code>now()</code> and no external call.</li>
  <li><strong>The model is disposable.</strong> Got corrupted? Delete and reprocess. It costs time (2 h 47 min for the 200 million), not data.</li>
  <li><strong>The bottleneck is writing, not reading.</strong> The Store delivers ~555 thousand events/s; the model takes 20,000/s.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

  <h4 class="deep-h">Corrections to the source article</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>"Projections are deterministic with respect to the current state".</strong> The sentence is confusing. The projection is deterministic with respect to the <strong>sequence of events</strong> (same input, same output), and that is why it is disposable. "Read Models are not just caches" is right: they are derived representations and can be rebuilt.</li>
    <li><strong>Projections "based on CQRS".</strong> The article ties the two together. In Fowler, Event Sourcing and CQRS are independent patterns: you can project without separating command and query models, and you can have CQRS without events as the truth.</li>
  </ul>

<p class="gancho">Rebuilding a whole model takes hours, and that is fine: it is rare. Day to day is different. Each <strong>command</strong> needs the state of <strong>one</strong> aggregate before it decides. What is left is how to rebuild <strong>a single</strong> aggregate, and what that costs.</p>
