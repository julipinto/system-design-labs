<p class="ponte">Last lesson rebuilding a whole <strong>model</strong> took hours, and that is fine, because it is rare. What happens all the time is smaller and more demanding: before accepting an <code>Enviar</code>, the <code>Pedido</code> aggregate needs to know what state 1047 is in. And the Store only keeps events. What is left is rebuilding its state right now, on every command. That job has a name: <strong>rehydrating</strong>.</p>

<OneLiner><strong>Rehydrating</strong> is rebuilding an aggregate's state by <strong>folding</strong> its events in order. It happens on <strong>every command</strong> (load the aggregate before deciding) and on every reprocessing. The cost grows with the <strong>size of the stream</strong>.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Starts empty and folds.</strong> The initial state of <code>pedido-1047</code> is nothing. <code>PedidoCriado</code> leads to <code>{`{status: criado, cliente: lena-77, total: 9490}`}</code>. <code>PedidoPago</code> changes the status to <code>pago</code>. <code>PedidoEnviado</code> sets <code>enviado</code> and the tracking <code>LA123456789BR</code>. <code>PedidoEntregue</code> leaves <code>entregue</code>. Result: <code>{`{pedido_id: 1047, status: entregue, total_centavos: 9490, rastreio: LA123456789BR, versao: 4}`}</code>. "Folding" is this: applying each event to the state, in order.</li>
  <li><strong>On every command.</strong> To decide whether <code>Enviar</code> is accepted, the <code>Pedido</code> folds its events and only then applies the rule (lesson 3). With 4 events it costs almost nothing. That is why the aggregate should be <strong>small</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>The cost is linear.</strong> On a stream of <strong>1,000,000 events</strong> (<code>estoque-CANECA-007</code>), reading everything in order and adding it up took <strong>1,935 · 1,801 · 1,770 ms</strong> in 3 runs: ~555 thousand events per second. Twice the events, twice the time.</li>
  <li><strong>To recover a domain.</strong> If <code>meus-pedidos</code> lost data, the application <strong>republishes</strong> the events so it can rebuild itself. That is the author's lab <code>rehydratate</code>, with an optional date filter.</li>
  <li><strong>To audit.</strong> "What was the status of 1047 at 14:15?" is folding only up to version 2 (<code>14:02:11</code>) and stopping: <code>pago</code>.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">The stream of the hot SKU</h3>

<p><code>estoque-CANECA-007</code> was built just to measure: for every 100 events, one <code>EstoqueAjustado +100</code> and 99 <code>EstoqueReservado −1</code>, and the final balance is <strong>10,000</strong>. Table and indexes take <strong>263 MB</strong> (~263 B per event, with a small payload). On Black Friday the blue mug gets ~20% of the 30 orders per second: <strong>6 events/s</strong> on the stream, <strong>86,400</strong> in 4 hours (8 PM to midnight). The million arrives in <strong>1.9 days</strong> of continuous peak (1,000,000 ÷ 6 ÷ 86,400), or months on ordinary days. Even so, by the end of that time, each command for the mug would carry <strong>1.8 s</strong> of rehydration. Lesson 8 fixes that.</p>

<h3 class="sec">What the author's lab does</h3>

<p>The lab's <code>rehydratate/listener.go</code> rehydrates to <strong>recover</strong>, not to decide. It receives a request (an <code>aggregate_id</code>, with optional dates), fetches <strong>all</strong> the aggregate's events ordered by version (lines 98 to 118) and folds, republishing the balance (lines 126 to 161). It was read, not executed.</p>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong</h3>

<p><strong>(a) Rehydrating with an event in an old format.</strong> The 2026 <code>PedidoCriado</code> recorded <code>frete</code> as a number (<code>1500</code>). A year later the code started expecting <code>frete_centavos</code>. The new code reads the old event, does not find the field and computes a wrong total. The event <strong>cannot be edited</strong>. The solution is <em>upcasting</em>: on read, convert the old event to the current format, before folding (Axon describes this pattern). The schema version goes in the event, and each format change gets a converter.</p>

<p><strong>(b) Republishing everything to a domain that is not idempotent.</strong> The recovery resends, for example, the 4 events of 1047 to <code>estoque</code>. If <code>estoque</code> applies each <code>PedidoPago</code> as "−1", the balance goes down <strong>again</strong>, as if the order had been placed once more. Rehydration is only safe if <strong>every consumer</strong> can receive the same event twice: lesson 10.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>Rehydrating is folding in order.</strong> The 4 events of 1047 give <code>entregue</code>, version 4; stopping at version 2 gives <code>pago</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>It happens on every command.</strong> That is why the aggregate has to be small and the stream cannot grow without limit.</li>
  <li><strong>The cost is linear.</strong> 1 million events take ~1,800 ms (measured, ~555 thousand/s).</li>
  <li><strong>It serves two uses.</strong> Deciding (command) and recovering (republishing for whoever lost data), and the second requires idempotent consumers.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
