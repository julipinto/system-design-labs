<p class="ponte">Last lesson the data got copies, but the problem of a bad slice was still open. The way out is the same as at the start: <strong>limit the radius</strong>, now with walls. Two walls, on different axes: <em>sharding</em> isolates <strong>data and customers</strong> and the <em>bulkhead</em> isolates <strong>resources</strong>, like pools and queues.</p>

<OneLiner><strong>Isolating</strong> is limiting the radius. <em>Sharding</em> isolates data and customers, the <strong><em>bulkhead</em></strong> (a ship's compartment) isolates resources: pools, queues, versions. The failure of one compartment does not flood the others.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The shared pool.</strong> <code>pagamentos</code> has a pool of <strong>100</strong> connections used by the PSP, the shipping quote and the email. With PSP-A at 8 s and 30 orders/s it fills in 100 ÷ 30 = <strong>3.33 s</strong>, and the quote and the email, which are fast, <strong>also</strong> get blocked.</li>
  <li><strong>The walls.</strong> With <em>bulkheads</em> of <strong>50</strong> (PSP), <strong>30</strong> (shipping quote) and <strong>20</strong> (email), only the PSP pool runs out, in 50 ÷ 30 = <strong>1.67 s</strong>. The other two keep going.</li>
  <li><strong>Other axes.</strong> One pool per <strong>dependency</strong>, per <strong>priority</strong> and per <strong>customer</strong>: <code>mkt-01</code> with its own queue.</li>
  <li><strong>Shuffle sharding.</strong> Module 16 drew 2 of 10 replicas for each customer: that is 45 pairs. The noisy customer only takes down whoever has the <strong>same pair</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">Sharding and bulkhead are not the same thing</h3>

<p>The article calls the <em>bulkhead</em> an "evolution of sharding". They are <strong>different axes</strong>: sharding cuts the data and the customers (the cube from module 15, the <code>shard-2</code> from lesson 3), and the bulkhead cuts resources and dependencies. <em>Shuffle sharding</em> is what ties the two together, because it cuts customers <em>and</em> resource compartments. The 1/N math keeps the caveat from lesson 3: <code>shard-2</code> takes 60% of the rows because of <code>mkt-01</code>. Module 26 takes the idea to the limit with cells per region.</p>

<h3 class="sec">And if it goes wrong: three walls that fail</h3>

<p>The first is the <strong>bulkhead that is too small</strong>: 30 connections for a peak of 40 in flight refuse legitimate traffic. The second is the <strong>leaky isolation</strong>: two separate pools that depend on the <strong>same</strong> database have the database's radius, and the wall is no use. The third is <strong>uneven load</strong>: <code>mkt-01</code> fills its slice and the wall is the wrong size.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>One pool, one radius.</strong> The shared pool puts PSP, shipping and email in the same compartment.</li>
  <li><strong>The walls cost a bit of capacity.</strong> 50/30/20 do not give the flexibility of 100 together, and a small bulkhead refuses the legitimate.</li>
  <li><strong>Shuffle sharding works on both axes.</strong> With 2 of 10, the noisy customer only reaches whoever has the same pair.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={4}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">The <em>lease</em>: a grant with a deadline</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>What it is.</strong> A <em>lease</em> is a grant <strong>with a deadline</strong> that the client must <strong>renew</strong>. If it does not renew, the resource goes back to the pool.</li>
    <li><strong>Where it shows up.</strong> In a connection pool with a <em>heartbeat</em>: the connection that stopped reporting is reclaimed. In a Kafka consumer with a partition: if the consumer stops reporting, the partition goes to another one.</li>
    <li><strong>Why it goes with the bulkhead.</strong> The PSP pool of 50 only recovers if the connections stuck for 8 s come back; the lease is what guarantees they do.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Correction to the source article: sharding × bulkhead and the lease</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>"The bulkhead is the evolution of sharding".</strong> They are different axes, and <em>shuffle sharding</em> is what ties the two together.</li>
