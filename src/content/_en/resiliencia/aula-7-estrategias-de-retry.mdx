<p class="ponte">Last lesson the timeout fired and the call is safe to repeat. What is left is deciding <strong>how</strong>. Trying again beats a passing failure, but it is what made 27 calls per tap in module 10. With no brake, the retry becomes the incident itself.</p>

<OneLiner><strong>Trying again</strong> beats passing failures, but <strong>with no brake it becomes the incident</strong>. The brake is exponential <em>backoff</em>, <em>jitter</em>, a budget and a deadline, and only on an idempotent operation.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Immediate, in memory.</strong> Simple: the same <em>thread</em> tries again. It solves most passing failures and it is what creates the storm when the dependency goes down.</li>
  <li><strong>Asynchronous.</strong> It answers <code>202 Accepted</code> instead of <code>201</code> and finishes later, or uses a queue with <em>ack</em> and a DLQ (module 13). The client does not wait for the retry.</li>
  <li><strong>Exponential <em>backoff</em>.</strong> The wait doubles on each failure, with a base of 100 ms: <strong>100, 200, 400, 800, 1,600 ms</strong>, with cumulative times at 0.1 · 0.3 · 0.7 · 1.5 · 3.1 s. The article uses 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 s; the store's cap is 2 s.</li>
  <li><strong><em>Jitter</em>.</strong> The wait is drawn at random, so clients do not come back together. <em>Full</em>: from 0 up to the cap for that round. <em>Equal</em>: half fixed plus half random. <em>Decorrelated</em>: a draw between the base and three times the previous wait.</li>
  <li><strong>The store's policy.</strong> Up to <strong>3 attempts</strong> (1 + 2 retries), only on <code>503</code>, <code>504</code>, timeout and connection refused, only on an operation with an <code>Idempotency-Key</code>, <em>backoff</em> with a 100 ms base and <em>full jitter</em> and a 2 s cap, and a <strong>10% budget</strong> of the calls. It only tries if there is deadline left.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">The storm in numbers</h3>

<p>A thousand clients call at the same time. The PSP is out for <strong>2.0 s</strong>, then serves <strong>200/s</strong> and refuses the rest. Each client tries until a 10 s deadline (base 100 ms, cap 5 s, seed 7):</p>

<p>The fixed interval gets everyone through, but with <strong>45.5 calls per client</strong>. Exponential <strong>without</strong> jitter sends synchronized waves of 1,000 and only <strong>4%</strong> finish within the deadline. Jitter spreads them out (peak of 84 to 130) and finishes almost everyone. The order matches AWS's: the one without jitter is "the clear loser". The difference between <em>full</em> and <em>equal</em> holds only in this scenario.</p>

<h3 class="sec">Amplification across layers</h3>

<p>Each layer repeats on its own and the attempts multiply. <strong>3 layers × 3 attempts = 27</strong> calls to <code>pagamentos</code> per tap (thread 2); 3 layers × 2 attempts, 8; one layer × 3, 3. At 30 orders per second that is <strong>810/s</strong> against a <code>pagamentos</code> with a capacity of <strong>40/s</strong>: <strong>20.2 times</strong> what it can handle. With a 10% budget, it is 30 × 1.1 = <strong>33/s</strong>. There are four defenses: repeat <strong>in one layer only</strong> (in the store, that of <code>pagamentos</code>, which repeats the PSP inside the 2.0 s deadline: the 2nd attempt goes out at 0.9 s), limit with a <strong>budget</strong> (the SRE book gives the example of 60 retries per minute per process), respect the <strong>deadline</strong> and repeat <strong>only what is idempotent</strong>.</p>

<h3 class="sec">And if it goes wrong: the retry that feeds the fire</h3>

<p>With no <em>backoff</em> or <em>jitter</em>, the retries arrive <strong>before</strong> the PSP recovers and <strong>afterwards</strong> in waves of 1,000. The PSP comes back in 90 s, but <code>pagamentos</code> stays overloaded by delayed retries and does not get out of the hole: it is a <strong>metastable</strong> failure, in which the extra recovery load keeps the system broken even without the original cause (described by Bronson and others at HotOS 2021; the PDF did not open during the check, so there is no link).</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The immediate retry is the cause of the storm.</strong> 1,000 clients become 45,500 calls.</li>
  <li><strong>Backoff alone is not enough.</strong> Without jitter the waves come back together and only 4% finish within the deadline.</li>
  <li><strong>The number of layers multiplies.</strong> 3 × 3 = 27: repeat in one layer, with a budget and a deadline.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

  <h4 class="deep-h">Correction to the source article: jitter, cap and immediate retry</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>"Incremental jitter" (0–4 s, 2–6 s, 6–10 s).</strong> It is none of the three AWS algorithms (<em>full</em>, <em>equal</em>, <em>decorrelated</em>). Missing are the <strong>cap</strong> on the wait and the warning that <em>backoff</em> without jitter creates synchronized waves (simulated above: 4% finish).</li>
    <li><strong>"The immediate retry solves most cases".</strong> When a dependency fails it is the <strong>cause</strong> of the storm (45,500 calls for 1,000 clients). Missing are amplification across layers (27), the budget, the deadline and "only idempotent".</li>
  </ul>
