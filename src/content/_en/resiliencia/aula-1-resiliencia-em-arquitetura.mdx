<OneLiner><strong>Resilience</strong> is staying up, <strong>fully or partially</strong>, when a piece fails. Nothing here is a new concept: it is <strong>a second look</strong> at what the course already showed, now through what each piece does when its neighbor is bad.</OneLiner>

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The path.</strong> One tap in the app crosses <code>app → gw-loja → bff-app → pedidos → pagamentos → PSP</code>. Under normal conditions it takes <strong>5 + 20 + 5 + 180 = 210 ms</strong> (module 12). Each arrow is a place where the call can be slow, repeat or fail.</li>
  <li><strong>The incident.</strong> At 20:20:00 <strong>PSP-A</strong> starts answering in 8 s. <code>pagamentos</code> calls PSP-A with a <strong>pool of 50 connections</strong> and receives <strong>30 orders/s</strong>, and it has <strong>2 replicas</strong> at 20/s each (capacity of <strong>40/s</strong>, module 14). A second PSP, <strong>PSP-B</strong>, answers with a p50 of 300 ms.</li>
  <li><strong>The star order.</strong> At <strong>20:20:04</strong> Caio, from Rio, pays order <strong>1058</strong>: 3 blue mugs (19.90 each = 59.70) + shipping to Rio (12.00) = <strong>71.70</strong>, by card. The mug stock goes from <strong>35 to 32</strong>. The events are <code>e-2101</code> <code>PedidoCriado</code>, <code>e-2102</code> <code>PedidoPago</code> and <code>e-2103</code> <code>EstoqueReservado</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>The defenses.</strong> Replicas and healthchecks, idempotency, timeout, retry, circuit breaker, fallback, degradation and backpressure, and then the data layer and isolation. Each one sits on its own link of the path and the next lessons add them one at a time.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">Resilience and availability are not the same thing</h3>

<p><strong>Availability</strong> measures how many good responses the store gave, or how long it stayed up. <strong>Resilience</strong> is the set of strategies that keeps that number high when something fails. One is the scoreboard, the other is the training. Lesson 2 teaches how to read the scoreboard; the other eleven are the training.</p>

<h3 class="sec">And if it goes wrong: the store with no defenses</h3>

<p>The slow PSP lasts 90 s, but without defenses the damage lasts longer. With every call stuck for 8 s and 30 orders/s coming in, the pool of 50 runs out in <strong>50 ÷ 30 = 1.67 s</strong>. With no free connection, the whole checkout stops, not just whoever would pay through PSP-A. The three layers also repeat the call and the count becomes <strong>27 per tap</strong>: 30 × 27 = <strong>810/s</strong> hitting a <code>pagamentos</code> that handles <strong>40/s</strong>. The storm keeps the service down even after the PSP comes back. In an example with <strong>5 minutes</strong> of checkout down, that is 9,000 failures, almost 70% of the month's error budget (lesson 2).</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>Resilience is a set of decisions, not a component.</strong> Each defense covers one link of the path and none covers it alone.</li>
  <li><strong>The worst incident is the one the defense makes worse.</strong> Repeating with no brake, waiting with no limit and ejecting a healthy replica turn 90 s of slow PSP into 5 min of store down.</li>
  <li><strong>Availability is the result.</strong> If you can't measure it (lesson 2), you can't say whether the defense worked.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
