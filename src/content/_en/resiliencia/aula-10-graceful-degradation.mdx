<p class="ponte">Last lesson the fallback swapped the <strong>path</strong>. But sometimes there is no better path: there are just too many people. At 20:20 a newsletter sends a burst of a few seconds at <strong>1,300 req/s</strong> to the catalog, which handles <strong>1,000</strong> (10 replicas × 100 req/s). What is left is deciding what to switch off so the essentials keep going.</p>

<OneLiner><strong>Degrading gracefully</strong> is <strong>switching off on purpose</strong> what is not essential to protect what is, <strong>before</strong> falling over. The fallback swaps the path; degradation reduces the <strong>functionality</strong>.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The burst.</strong> The demand of <strong>1,300 req/s</strong> against a capacity of <strong>1,000</strong> lasts a few seconds. It is not the sustained peak: module 14 measured 640 req/s and projected 950. <em>Autoscaling</em> (HPA, from 10 to 14 replicas = 1,400 req/s) takes minutes, and the burst is over before that.</li>
  <li><strong>Level 0.</strong> Normal: 10 ms of CPU per page, 100 req/s per replica, <strong>1,000</strong> in total.</li>
  <li><strong>Level 1.</strong> No recommendations: 7.5 ms, 133 req/s per replica, <strong>1,333</strong>. That already covers the 1,300.</li>
  <li><strong>Level 2.</strong> Plus simplified search and no reviews: 6 ms, 167 req/s per replica, <strong>1,667</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>Level 3.</strong> Only the static page from the CDN (module 5). The whole store leaves the server and the CDN holds the storefront.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">Who goes first</h3>

<p>The SRE book names the classes: <code>CRITICAL_PLUS</code>, <code>CRITICAL</code>, <code>SHEDDABLE_PLUS</code> and <code>SHEDDABLE</code>. For the store the order is <strong>checkout</strong> › <strong>catalog</strong> › <strong>recommendations and "my orders"</strong> › <strong>reports</strong>. Degradation switches off from the bottom up, and checkout only comes in at the end.</p>

<p>The trigger can be <strong>manual</strong> (the <em>feature toggle</em> from module 9) or <strong>automatic</strong> (a saturation signal from module 14). Fallback and degradation are relatives: the article separates them as "after the failure" and "before", but both can be automatic or manual. What tells them apart is <strong>what is sacrificed</strong>: the path or the functionality.</p>

<h3 class="sec">And if it goes wrong: three degradations that fail</h3>

<p>The first is <strong>degrading late</strong>: with the pool already saturated, there is no way to switch anything on or off, because the configuration is read from the service that went down. The second is the <strong>switch that does not come back</strong>: the <em>toggle</em> stays at level 2 after the peak, and the store sells without reviews for weeks. Define <strong>hysteresis</strong> (go back only after a while below the limit) and an owner. The third is <strong>degrading the essential by mistake</strong>: search switched off along with the recommendations, and nobody can find the product.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>Switching off on purpose buys time.</strong> Level 1 (1,333) holds the 1,300 while the HPA takes minutes.</li>
  <li><strong>Priority is a product decision.</strong> Checkout, catalog, recommendations and reports, in that order.</li>
  <li><strong>Every degradation needs a way back.</strong> Hysteresis and an owner, otherwise the temporary becomes permanent.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
