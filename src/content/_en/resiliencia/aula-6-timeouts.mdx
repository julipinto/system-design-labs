<p class="ponte">Last lesson the repeated call became safe. What is left is <strong>when to repeat</strong>, and for that you have to decide when to <strong>stop waiting</strong>. With no limit, a call stuck for 8 s holds one connection of the <code>pagamentos</code> pool of 50 for 8 s. At 30 orders per second, the pool runs out before the first response arrives.</p>

<OneLiner>A <strong>timeout</strong> is the limit that keeps a slow call from <strong>getting stuck</strong> and exhausting the pool. The right value comes from a <strong>percentile</strong> and a <strong>capacity calculation</strong>, not from a round number.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The types.</strong> The <strong>connection</strong> timeout limits opening the channel; the <strong>read/write</strong> one limits waiting for the bytes; the <strong>idle</strong> one drops a connection that has gone quiet.</li>
  <li><strong>Choose by percentile.</strong> AWS says to accept a small fraction of false timeouts and look at the matching percentile. PSP-A has a p50 of <strong>180 ms</strong>, a p99 of <strong>400 ms</strong> and a p99.9 of <strong>700 ms</strong>. Accepting 0.1% false timeouts, the timeout is the p99.9 plus some slack: <strong>1 s</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>Check with Little.</strong> The number of calls in flight is <strong>rate × time</strong>. With a pool of 50 and 30 req/s, the critical timeout is <strong>50 ÷ 30 = 1.67 s</strong>: above that the pool saturates.</li>
  <li><strong>Chain the deadlines.</strong> Each outer link waits <strong>longer</strong> than the inner one: app <strong>5 s</strong> › <code>gw-loja</code> <strong>3 s</strong> › <code>bff-app</code> <strong>2.8 s</strong> › <code>pedidos</code> <strong>2.5 s</strong> › <code>pagamentos</code> <strong>2.0 s</strong> › PSP <strong>1.0 s per attempt</strong>.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">Little's math, with the store's pool</h3>

<p>With a pool of 50 and 30 req/s, the time of each call decides everything. Under normal conditions (180 ms), 5.4 calls are in flight. At 0.6 s, 18. At <strong>1.0 s</strong>, 30: it fits. At <strong>2.0 s</strong>, 60: it <strong>saturates</strong>. At <strong>8.0 s</strong>, 240. The maximum throughput of the pool of 50 is 277/s at 180 ms, <strong>50/s</strong> at 1 s, <strong>25/s</strong> at 2 s and <strong>6.25/s</strong> at 8 s. At 8 s the pool runs out in <strong>1.67 s</strong>, and at 2 s too: 60 is more than 50.</p>

<h3 class="sec">Why the module 10 timeout changed from 2 s to 1 s</h3>

<p>In module 10 <code>pagamentos</code> used a <strong>2 s</strong> timeout. It looked generous: 11 times the normal latency. Little's math shows it was not. At 30 orders per second, a 2 s timeout keeps <strong>60</strong> calls in flight in a pool of <strong>50</strong>: the pool fills up and the queue grows. That value was chosen without the capacity calculation. The new one, <strong>1 s</strong>, comes from the PSP's p99.9 (700 ms) plus slack and keeps 30 in flight, inside the pool. The same goes for the whole <code>pagamentos</code>: the PSP can use up to 1 s <strong>per attempt</strong>, and <code>pagamentos</code> still has 2.0 s of total deadline.</p>

<h3 class="sec">The timeout does not cancel the work on the other side</h3>

<p>In module 12 the PSP took 8 s, <code>pagamentos</code> gave up after 2 s and charge <code>c-7731</code> <strong>already existed</strong> at the PSP. The timeout only says "I stopped waiting", not "it did not happen". That is why idempotency (lesson 5) comes first and why the saga (module 19) exists afterwards: whoever gives up needs a way to find out what got done.</p>

<h3 class="sec">And if it goes wrong: the outer one gives up and the inner one keeps going</h3>

<p>The sum of the deadlines in a sequence of attempts can exceed the caller's deadline. Three 1 s attempts plus waits of 0.1 s and 0.2 s add up to <strong>3.3 s</strong>, more than the <strong>3 s</strong> of <code>gw-loja</code>. <code>gw-loja</code> gives up, <code>pagamentos</code> keeps working and the result goes to nobody: that is <strong>orphaned work</strong>. The fix is for the second attempt to receive only what is left of the deadline: 2.0 − 1.0 − 0.1 = <strong>0.9 s</strong>.</p>

<p>The second mistake is a timeout that is <strong>too long for the pool</strong>, which is the case of the 2 s from module 10: the pool fills up and the queue grows without the store having a single business failure.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>A timeout comes from a calculation.</strong> The percentile gives the sensible value; Little says whether the pool can take it.</li>
  <li><strong>The outer one waits longer than the inner one.</strong> When it is inverted, the outer one gives up and the inner one works for nobody.</li>
  <li><strong>Giving up is not undoing.</strong> After the timeout the operation may have happened: c-7731 already existed.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
