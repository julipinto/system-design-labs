<p class="ponte">Last lesson the retry had a brake, but it still believed PSP-A would come back. It stays at 8 s for 90 s and, meanwhile, each call spends a connection and a whole timeout to fail. The one who has to decide to <strong>stop calling</strong> is a breaker.</p>

<OneLiner>The <strong><em>circuit breaker</em></strong> <strong>stops calling</strong> a dependency that is failing, <strong>fails fast</strong> and tests it now and then to find out whether it is back.</OneLiner>

<DoIt>
  <p>In the figure, in the <strong>Breaker</strong> scenario, leave PSP-A at <strong>8 s</strong> and read the timeline: it opens at <strong>1.3 s</strong>, probes at 31.3 and 62.47 s, closes at <strong>93.91 s</strong>. Compare the calls to the PSP: <strong>237</strong> against 3,000 without the breaker.</p>
</DoIt>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Closed.</strong> The call goes through and the breaker counts the result in a window. Here the window is <strong>20</strong> calls and the minimum to decide is <strong>10</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>Opens.</strong> With <strong>50% or more</strong> failures, the breaker opens. A <strong>slow</strong> call (over 1 s) counts as a failure. Once open, nothing goes through for <strong>30 s</strong>: the response comes back immediately, that is the <em>fast fail</em>.</li>
  <li><strong>Half-open.</strong> After 30 s it lets <strong>5 probes</strong> through. If the probes come back fine, it closes. If they fail, it opens again for another 30 s.</li>
  <li><strong>One per dependency.</strong> PSP-A and PSP-B each have their own breaker. The state of the PSP-A breaker is what <strong>triggers the fallback</strong> to PSP-B (lesson 9).</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<p>In 100 s PSP-A received <strong>237 calls</strong> (<strong>54</strong> until the breaker closed and <strong>183</strong> after) <strong>against 3,000</strong> without a breaker, of which 2,700 were useless. The remaining <strong>2,763</strong> were fast fails. Without a breaker, with a 1 s timeout the pool reaches at most 31 in flight; with a 2 s timeout, <strong>484</strong> calls are refused by the pool (peak of 50); with 8 s, <strong>2,260</strong>.</p>

<h3 class="sec">The library defaults and the store's configuration</h3>

<p>Resilience4j sets the defaults <code>failureRateThreshold</code> of 50, <code>slidingWindowSize</code> of 100, <code>minimumNumberOfCalls</code> of 100, <code>waitDurationInOpenState</code> of 60 s, <code>permittedNumberOfCallsInHalfOpenState</code> of 10 and <code>slowCallDurationThreshold</code> of 60 s. <code>pagamentos</code> uses a much smaller window (20, minimum 10), open for 30 s, 5 probes and slow = over 1 s, because a window of 100 calls at 30/s only decides after 3.3 s of failure.</p>

<h3 class="sec">And if it goes wrong: three ways the breaker does not help</h3>

<p>The first is <strong>counting only failures, not slowness</strong>: the PSP answers in 8 s "successfully", the breaker never sees an error and never opens. That is why a slow call counts as a failure. The second is the <strong>long timeout that delays the breaker</strong>: with a 2 s timeout it only opens at 2.3 s and 11 calls are still refused by the pool. The third is the <strong>window that is too small</strong>, which opens on noise: 5 calls, 2 failures, 40%, and one more is enough to open for no reason.</p>

<Widget name="breaker" title="Circuit breaker" hint="Window, minimum, threshold, open time, probes and timeout. The timeline shows the states; the numbers compare with the scenario without a breaker (opens at 1.3 s; 237 × 3,000 calls)." />

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The breaker fails fast.</strong> 2,763 calls come back immediately instead of holding a connection for 1 s.</li>
  <li><strong>The pool is grateful.</strong> 237 calls to the PSP instead of 3,000: the pool never goes past 31 in flight.</li>
  <li><strong>Slow is failure.</strong> A breaker that only counts errors does not protect against a PSP that takes too long.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
