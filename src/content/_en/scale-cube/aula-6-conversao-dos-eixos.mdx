<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>The store moves through the cube in the order things hurt. <strong>(1, 1, 1)</strong> is the monolith from module 5. Y comes first, in module 6 (7 services) and in modules 8 and 9 (the gateway and the BFFs: 7 services + <code>bff-app</code> + <code>bff-web</code> = 9 deployments): <strong>(1, 9, 1)</strong>. Then X, with the load balancer from module 7 and the Black Friday from module 14: <strong>(10, 9, 1)</strong>. And Z, in module 16, only on <code>pedidos</code>: <strong>(10, 9, 3)</strong>.</li>
  <li>The deploy unit math is the sum, per service, of replicas × partitions. Today that is <strong>30 pods</strong>: catalogo 10, carrinho 3, pedidos 3, pagamentos 2, estoque 2, entregas 1, relatorios 1, bff-app 4 and bff-web 4. With Z on <code>pedidos</code> (3 shards × 3 replicas = 9 pods instead of 3), that is 30 − 3 + 9 = <strong>36</strong>.</li>
  <li>Inside each Z partition, X still applies: each shard has its replicas and the replicas of its database (module 17). AKF calls this set a <strong>pod</strong>, their word, which is not the Kubernetes pod. It is the pods-per-shard model from the last lesson, where each database sees only 3 × 10 = 30 connections.</li>
  <li>The conversion goes both ways. From <strong>symptom to axis</strong>, with the table from lesson 1. From <strong>capacity (req/s) to number of pods</strong>: with 200 orders/s and 30/s per pod, ⌈200 ÷ 30⌉ = 7 <code>pedidos</code> pods, and the database still stops at 150. With 3 shards, each one receives 66.7/s, and 3 pods per shard (90/s) leave headroom.</li>
  <li>Per state, the table closes the story: <strong>(3, 1, 1)</strong> outputs <strong>90/s</strong>, <strong>(7, 1, 1)</strong> outputs <strong>150/s</strong> and <strong>(3, 1, 3)</strong> outputs <strong>200/s</strong>. The widget below lets you change the three numbers and see the limiting link.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: all three axes at once</h3>

<p>Someone reads the cube as a shopping list. The catalog already has 10 replicas (X). Then they split the search into 4 microservices (Y) and add 3 partitions (Z). The product is 10 × 4 × 3 = <strong>120 pods</strong> for a service that delivered <strong>100 req/s per replica</strong>. The cost of CPU, of network between the pods and of operations grows with the <strong>product</strong> of the axes, and the original symptom (CPU at 99%) never asked for Y or Z. Each axis is used when the table's symptom points to it.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The order is the bottleneck's.</strong> The store moved Y, X and only now Z, in that sequence, because each step was the one that hurt.</li>
  <li><strong>The axes multiply.</strong> Pods = per-service sum of X × Z: 30 today, 36 with Z on <code>pedidos</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>The math converts both ways.</strong> From symptom to axis, and from capacity (req/s) to number of pods.</li>
  <li><strong>Using everything at once costs.</strong> 10 × 4 × 3 = 120 pods for a catalog that delivered 100 req/s per replica.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
