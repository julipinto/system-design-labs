<p class="ponte">Last lesson the store decided to keep events in a log, for 7 days, so any group can read from its own point. That is what Kafka does. But a log on a single server would be a single point of failure and a bottleneck, and Black Friday reaches 120 events per second. To fit the log across several brokers and several consumers, Kafka splits it into <strong>partitions</strong> and copies each one. Let's go through the <code>pedidos</code> topic piece by piece.</p>

<OneLiner>The topic is an <strong>append-only log split into partitions</strong>; the <strong>offset</strong> is the position of each event in the partition; the <strong>key</strong> decides the partition, and each <strong>consumer group</strong> keeps its own offset.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>The <code>pedidos</code> topic has <strong>3 partitions</strong> (p0, p1, p2) and <strong>replication factor 3</strong>, on the brokers <code>kafka-1</code>, <code>kafka-2</code> and <code>kafka-3</code>. This is what the <code>kafka-topics --describe</code> command shows: each partition with its leader, its replicas and the ISR, the set of in-sync replicas.<pre><code>{`Topic: pedidos  PartitionCount: 3  ReplicationFactor: 3
  Partition: 0  Leader: 1  Replicas: 1,2,3  Isr: 1,2,3
  Partition: 1  Leader: 2  Replicas: 2,3,1  Isr: 2,3,1
  Partition: 2  Leader: 3  Replicas: 3,1,2  Isr: 3,1,2`}</code></pre></li>
  <li>The <strong>producer</strong> sends each event with a <strong>key</strong>, the <code>pedido_id</code>, and the client computes <code>murmur2(key) mod 3</code>. All the events of 1051 land on <strong>p0</strong>, <strong>in order</strong>: offsets 48210, 48213, 48217 and 48301. Without a key, the client picks the partition (per batch, in the Java client). Order only holds <strong>within the partition</strong>; between partitions there is no order.</li>
  <li>The producer also decides durability with <code>acks</code>: <code>0</code> waits for nobody, <code>1</code> waits only for the leader, <code>all</code> waits for everyone in the ISR (the default since Kafka 3.0, together with <code>enable.idempotence = true</code>). And it decides the batch: <code>batch.size</code> is in <strong>bytes</strong> (default 16384) and <code>linger.ms</code> is the time to collect (5 ms since Kafka 4.0).</li>
  <li>The <strong>consumer</strong> belongs to a <strong>group</strong>, and each group keeps <strong>its own</strong> offset in each partition. That is why <code>estoque</code>, <code>entregas</code> and <code>notificacoes</code> read everything, each at its own pace. Inside the group, each partition goes to <strong>one</strong> consumer, so the <strong>maximum number of useful consumers is the number of partitions</strong>: the <code>entregas</code> group with 5 replicas and 3 partitions has 2 idle.<pre><code>{`GROUP          TOPIC   PARTITION  CURRENT-OFFSET  LOG-END-OFFSET  LAG
estoque        pedidos 0          48220           48220           0
notificacoes   pedidos 0          48219           48220           1
entregas       pedidos 0          48210           48220           10`}</code></pre></li>
  <li>Same key, same partition, <strong>one consumer at a time</strong>: the events of 1051 are read in order by a single consumer of the group, <strong>with no mutex or lock</strong>. It is the partition doing the work of module 11's lock. For events to survive an outage, each partition has 1 <strong>leader</strong>, which takes writes and reads, and 2 <strong>followers</strong>, which copy. With <code>acks=all</code> and <code>min.insync.replicas = 2</code>, the producer only gets the confirmation when at least 2 replicas have the event. The metadata lives in Kafka itself, <strong>KRaft</strong>, and ZooKeeper left in the 4.x series.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the leader goes down before the follower copies</h3>

<p>With <code>acks=1</code>, the leader of <strong>p0</strong>, <code>kafka-1</code>, confirms Lena's <code>PedidoPago</code> (offset 48213) at t = 0 and <strong>goes down at t = 5 ms</strong>, before the follower copies, which takes about 10 ms. <code>kafka-2</code> becomes the leader <strong>without the event</strong>. The producer thinks it sent, <code>estoque</code> never sees the payment and Lena is left without her mugs.</p>

<p>With <code>acks=all</code>, <code>kafka-1</code> does not confirm before at least 2 replicas have the event. It goes down at 5 ms, without confirming. The producer, with no confirmation, <strong>retries</strong>, and the event goes into the new leader (idempotence prevents it from going in twice). If <strong>two</strong> brokers go down, the ISR is left at {'{3}'}, smaller than 2, and the producer gets <code>NotEnoughReplicas</code> and <strong>stops accepting</strong>. The store loses availability to avoid losing data, the CAP choice from module 3.</p>

<Widget name="acks" title="Loss with acks: the leader goes down at t ms" hint="Pick acks (0, 1 or all), min.insync.replicas (1 to 3), how many replicas are in sync and when the leader goes down (0 to 50 ms). <p>How long does the election take? In an abrupt outage, the KRaft controller only takes the broker out when its lease expires: <code>broker.session.timeout.ms</code> is <strong>9000 ms</strong> (the default in the 4.0 series documentation), with heartbeats every 2000 ms. So, as a worked example of those settings, p0 is <strong>without a leader for up to about 9 s</strong> and the producer retries until <code>delivery.timeout.ms</code> (2 minutes by default). In a controlled shutdown, the leader changes beforehand, and the pause is much smaller. The real number depends on your cluster.</p>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the group is at its limit and the partitions run out</h3>

<p>The <code>entregas</code> group processes each event in 100 ms, that is, <strong>10 per second</strong> per consumer. On Black Friday, 30 relevant events per second arrive (<code>EstoqueReservado</code>), and 30 ÷ 3 consumers = 10 per second each, <strong>exactly the limit</strong>. Any burst turns into lag. Adding consumer replicas <strong>does not help</strong>: with 3 partitions, more than 3 consumers sit idle.</p>

<p>Going to <strong>6 partitions</strong> helps, but it changes the <code>hash mod 6</code>: key <strong>1051 moves from p0 to p3</strong> (and 1049, from p0 to p3). During the transition, events of the same order sit in two partitions, read by two consumers, and <strong>lose their order</strong>: <code>PedidoEnviado</code> can be read before <code>PedidoPago</code>.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The key chooses what stays in order.</strong> With <code>pedido_id</code> as the key, the 4 events of 1051 come out of p0 in order.</li>
  <li><strong>Each group reads at its own pace.</strong> The 3 groups have their own offsets: lag 0, 1 and 10 at the same instant.</li>
  <li><strong>Partitions limit the consumers.</strong> 5 replicas on 3 partitions leave 2 idle, and 6 partitions change the partition of the keys.</li>
  <li><strong><code>acks=all</code> only protects together with <code>min.insync.replicas</code>.</strong> With <code>acks=1</code>, Lena's <code>PedidoPago</code> can vanish in 5 ms.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

  <p>The Java client's default partitioner does <code>murmur2(key) mod n</code>, with the sign bit cleared before the <code>mod</code>. The function was reimplemented and checked against Kafka's test vectors (<code>"21"</code> gives −973932308 and <code>"foobar"</code> gives −790332482). Orders 1048 and 1050 stay on p2 in both cases: only the partition of those landing on a different remainder changes.</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Delivery: at-least-once and the DLQ that does not exist</h4>
  <p>Kafka's default for the consumer is <strong>at-least-once</strong>: if the consumer goes down after processing and before writing the offset, the event is read again. <em>Exactly-once</em> requires idempotence in the consumer (an <code>evento_id</code>, like the repeated <code>e-502</code> from module 6) or transactions. And Kafka has <strong>no native DLQ</strong> for consumers: the team creates the <code>pedidos.dlq</code> topic and the logic to send messages there (Kafka Connect has its own).</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">The election time, with sources</h4>
  <p>The numbers come from the <a href="https://kafka.apache.org/40/generated/kafka_config.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">4.0 series configuration documentation</a>: <code>broker.session.timeout.ms</code> = 9000 (the lifetime of a broker lease without heartbeats), <code>broker.heartbeat.interval.ms</code> = 2000, <code>replica.lag.time.max.ms</code> = 30000 (a follower that does not fetch data for that long leaves the ISR) and, for the election of the KRaft quorum itself, <code>controller.quorum.election.timeout.ms</code> = 1000 and <code>controller.quorum.fetch.timeout.ms</code> = 2000. The lesson's "up to about 9 s" is a worked example using those defaults, not a measurement.</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Corrections to the source article</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong><code>acks</code>.</strong> The article speaks of "volume of ACKs" and "the higher the number of ACKs". In Kafka the values are <code>0</code>, <code>1</code> and <code>all</code>, and <code>all</code> only really protects together with <code>min.insync.replicas</code>. The article's example uses <code>"acks": "1"</code> with the comment "guarantee durability": <code>1</code> does <strong>not</strong> give that guarantee, as the scenario above shows.</li>
    <li><strong>A load balancer in front of Kafka.</strong> The article says load across brokers can be handled by a load balancer or a CNAME. The client fetches the metadata and connects <strong>directly to the leader of each partition</strong> (the address in <code>advertised.listeners</code>); a layer 4 balancer in front usually breaks that. The recommended setup is a <code>bootstrap.servers</code> list. And "all brokers receive any event" only holds for the first contact: the write goes to the partition's <strong>leader</strong>.</li>
    <li><strong>"Random" partition without a key.</strong> Since Kafka 2.4 (KIP-480) the Java client uses a <strong>sticky</strong> partition: a whole batch goes to the same partition. In <code>librdkafka</code>, the "any" partition uses the default partitioner (<code>consistent_random</code>). There is no guaranteed "uniform" distribution.</li>
    <li><strong>What is missing from the article's Kafka.</strong> Offsets and commit, retention and compaction, ISR and <code>min.insync.replicas</code>, rebalance, the delivery semantics (at-most, at-least and exactly-once), KRaft (ZooKeeper was removed in version 4.0) and the absence of a native DLQ. The consumer in the article's example also acknowledges <strong>on every message</strong> (slow) and <strong>stops at the first error</strong>, and <code>ReadMessage(-1)</code> blocks forever.</li>
  </ul>
