<p class="ponte">Last lesson the truck <code>caminhao-17</code> published its position to a topic and <code>rastreio</code> subscribed to a filter, with QoS, retained and Last Will. <OneLiner>In the lab, a producer publishes one message per second with <strong>QoS 1</strong> and a consumer subscribes to the same topic with a <strong>clean session</strong>: everything the consumer misses while it is away is <strong>lost for good</strong>.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>The <code>messages/mqtt/default/</code> folder has a <code>docker-compose.yml</code> with <code>eclipse-mosquitto:2.0</code>, on ports <strong>1883</strong> (MQTT) and <strong>9001</strong>, and a 4-line <code>mosquitto.conf</code>: <code>listener 1883</code>, <code>allow_anonymous true</code>, <code>persistence true</code> and the persistence location.</li>
  <li><code>producer.go</code> connects to <code>tcp://localhost:1883</code> with the <code>clientID</code> <code>producer-&lt;unix&gt;</code>, turns on <code>SetAutoReconnect(true)</code> and publishes <code>Mensagem #N - &lt;time&gt;</code> with <strong>QoS 1</strong> to the lab's topic, <code>linuxtips/iot</code>, <strong>every 1 s</strong>. After each <code>Publish</code>, it waits for the token: that is lesson 7's <code>PUBACK</code>.</li>
  <li><code>consumer.go</code> subscribes to the same topic with QoS 1 and <code>SetCleanSession(true)</code>. A clean session means that, on disconnect, the broker <strong>forgets</strong> the subscription and its pending messages.</li>
  <li>The mapping to the store is one to one: the topic <code>linuxtips/iot</code> becomes <code>loja/entregas/caminhao-17/posicao</code>, and the text string becomes the 38-byte JSON <code>&#123;"lat":-8.0476,"lng":-34.877,"vel":62&#125;</code>. The rest, the producer with QoS 1 and the consumer that subscribes to a topic, is exactly the design of <code>rastreio</code>.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">Five divergences between the lab and the lesson</h3>

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The README and the code contradict each other.</strong> The <code>README</code> talks about the topic <code>linuxtips/demo</code> and an interval of <strong>5 s</strong>; the code uses <code>linuxtips/iot</code> and <strong>1 s</strong> (the code's comment also says 5 s, and the loop waits 1 s).</li>
  <li><strong>The compose only brings up the broker.</strong> The <code>README</code> says <code>docker compose up</code> brings up producer and consumer; the <code>docker-compose.yml</code> only brings up <strong>Mosquitto</strong>. Producer and consumer run by hand, with <code>go run</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>The configuration is lab-only.</strong> The compose exposes port 9001 (WebSocket), but <code>mosquitto.conf</code> only declares the <strong>1883</strong> listener; and <code>allow_anonymous true</code> accepts anyone. In production, user and password, per-topic ACLs and TLS on port 8883.</li>
  <li><strong>The client is MQTT 3.1.1.</strong> <code>paho.mqtt.golang v1.4.3</code> speaks 3.1.1. Shared subscription (<code>$share</code>) is from <strong>MQTT 5.0</strong> and needs another client, <code>eclipse/paho.golang</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>The <code>clientID</code> changes on every run.</strong> With <code>time.Now().Unix()</code>, the consumer is never the same one, and <strong>you cannot resume a persistent session</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the consumer starts after the producer</h3>

<p>The producer starts publishing and the consumer comes up minutes later. With <code>CleanSession(true)</code>, the messages sent while it was away are <strong>lost</strong>, and the <code>README</code> itself warns about it. To keep them, you need <code>CleanSession(false)</code>, a <strong>fixed</strong> <code>clientID</code> and QoS 1: the broker keeps the subscriber's messages while it is away.</p>

<p>That keeping has a limit. In 10 minutes, at 1 message per second, that is <strong>600</strong> messages. Mosquitto's default per-client queue limit is <strong>1,000</strong> (<code>max_queued_messages</code>, checked in the documentation). With 20 minutes, it is <strong>1,200</strong>, and the 200 extra ones are dropped. A persistent session is not a log: it is a short queue per subscriber.</p>

<p>These numbers were tested. With the compose's Mosquitto 2.0, without touching the configuration, a subscriber with a fixed <code>clientID</code>, a persistent session and QoS 1 went offline, and I published messages with <code>mosquitto_pub</code>: of the 600, <strong>600</strong> came back; of the 1,200, <strong>1,000</strong> came back. The ones that stayed were the first 1,000 (1 to 1000), and the <strong>200 newest</strong> (1001 to 1200) were the ones dropped. With a clean session, 10 published and none delivered on return. This matters for tracking: when the queue fills up, what is lost is the truck's most recent position, precisely the one it wants.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>A clean session forgets.</strong> What goes by while the consumer is away is gone.</li>
  <li><strong>A persistent session needs a fixed <code>clientID</code>.</strong> The lab generates a new one on every run.</li>
  <li><strong>The session queue has a ceiling.</strong> 600 fit in the 1,000; 1,200 lose 200.</li>
  <li><strong>The lab is the base, not production.</strong> No authentication, no TLS and on 3.1.1.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
