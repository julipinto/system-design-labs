<p class="ponte">Module 12 ended with Davi's order <strong>1050</strong> paid: the PSP called the store's webhook and <code>PedidoPago</code> went out. But that notice has to reach three places, <code>estoque</code>, <code>entregas</code> and <code>notificacoes</code>, and every call in the last module waits for someone. Making the three calls one after another slows the payment down, and if <code>entregas</code> is down, the payment fails. The problem in this module is <strong>how to notify without waiting, without losing the notice and without the producer knowing who listens</strong>.</p>

<OneLiner>Asynchronous communication means handing the notice to an intermediary, the <strong>broker</strong>, and moving on: whoever needs it reads it when they can, and the sender neither waits for an answer nor knows who reads.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>Today, when Lena's payment comes in, <code>pedidos</code> calls <code>estoque</code> (20 ms), then <code>entregas</code> (100 ms), then <code>notificacoes</code> (10 ms), one at a time. The response takes <strong>130 ms longer</strong>, and Lena stares at the screen while the store notifies people who have nothing to do with her answer.</li>
  <li>The second cost is dependency. If <code>entregas</code> is down, the second call fails and <strong>the order fails with it</strong>, even though the payment already happened. A synchronous call ties the caller's time to the time of whoever answers.</li>
  <li>The asynchronous path replaces the three calls with one. <code>pedidos</code> publishes <strong>1 event</strong> to the Kafka topic <code>pedidos</code> and waits only for the broker to confirm it wrote it: about <strong>10 ms</strong> with <code>acks=all</code>. Then it answers Lena.</li>
  <li>The three services read the topic whenever they want. Did <code>entregas</code> go down? <code>PedidoPago</code> waits stored in the log and is read when they come back. This is the idea module 6 already gave, "wait in the queue", now with a real place to wait.</li>
  <li>The sender also stops knowing who listens. Without events, <code>pedidos</code> knows <strong>3 destinations</strong>; with events, it knows <strong>1 topic</strong>. A fourth interested party, an <code>antifraude</code> service, starts reading the same topic without touching <code>pedidos</code>.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the synchronous chain with entregas down</h3>

<p>It is 14:20:03 and the PSP reports that Lena paid order 1051, for 54.80. <code>pedidos</code> calls <code>estoque</code> and it works. The call to <code>entregas</code> never comes back, because the service is under maintenance, and <code>pedidos</code> returns an error. Lena's money already left, the order did not move forward and <code>notificacoes</code> was never called.</p>

<p>With the event in Kafka, the same outage becomes a delay: <code>PedidoPago</code> stays in the topic, <code>entregas</code> comes back and reads it. The new design has a different price, and it shows up in the next lessons: the response to Lena no longer says whether the stock was reserved, because that happens <em>afterwards</em>. Her screen shows "paid" and the rest arrives later.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The payment stops waiting for its neighbors.</strong> The response drops from +130 ms to about 10 ms of publishing.</li>
  <li><strong>An outage does not take the order down.</strong> The notice waits in the log until the service returns.</li>
  <li><strong>The producer stays light.</strong> It knows 1 topic, not 3 services and their addresses.</li>
  <li><strong>It has a cost.</strong> The result arrives after the response, and you have to handle notices that are duplicated, out of order or late (lessons 5 to 10).</li>
</WhyItMatters>

  <p>A synchronous call is still the right choice when the screen needs the answer to continue, like looking up the cat's price or checking a password. Asynchronous fits what can happen later, like decrementing stock, preparing the delivery and sending the email.</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">The numbers in the example</h4>
  <p>The times of 20, 100 and 10 ms are examples, chosen so the arithmetic is easy to see. So is the 10 ms for the broker to confirm with <code>acks=all</code>: it depends on the network between the brokers and on the disk. What does not depend on any number is the structure: the sum becomes a single step.</p>
