<p class="ponte">Last lesson the email topology was on paper: a topic exchange <code>emails</code>, three queues, one DLX. The author's public repository has three Go labs with RabbitMQ that set up exactly the three ways of routing: <code>default/</code> (direct), <code>topic/</code> and <code>fanout/</code>. Let's read them against the lesson, because each one leaves out something that lesson 9's guarantee called for.</p>

<OneLiner>The three labs show routing by <strong>direct</strong> (one queue), <strong>topic</strong> (patterns) and <strong>fanout</strong> (copy to all), but none has a <strong>persistent message</strong>, <strong>prefetch</strong> or <strong>publisher confirms</strong>: each one's messages are lost if the broker restarts.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong><code>default/</code></strong> declares the exchange <code>ecommerce.nova.venda</code> (direct, durable), the queue <code>cobrar</code> and the binding <code>cobrar</code>. The consumer reads with <code>auto-ack = false</code> and acknowledges with <code>d.Ack(true)</code>. It is the design of a command to a single recipient, like lesson 2's <code>CobrarPedido</code>.</li>
  <li><strong><code>topic/</code></strong> declares the exchange <code>ecommerce.nova.venda.faturamento</code> (topic) and three queues: <code>queue.faturamento</code> (binding <code>faturamento.prioridade.default</code>), <code>queue.faturamento.prioritario</code> (<code>faturamento.prioridade.alta</code>) and <code>queue.faturamento.datalake</code> (<code>faturamento.prioridade.*</code>). The producer sends 20 messages, with a 10% chance of high priority, and the datalake receives <strong>all 20</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong><code>fanout/</code></strong> declares an exchange <code>ecommerce.nova.venda</code> of type fanout with the queues <code>cobrar_pedido</code>, <code>reservar_estoque</code> and <code>informar_logistica</code>. The routing key is ignored, and <strong>each of the 3 queues receives every message</strong>. The code publishes <strong>1,000</strong> (the loop is <code>i &lt; 1000</code>).</li>
  <li>The mapping to the store: <code>prioridade.alta</code> is <code>email.pedido.*</code> (transactional); <code>prioridade.default</code> is <code>email.promo.*</code>; the datalake is <code>emails.auditoria</code>, which gets a copy of everything. The <code>fanout</code> is <code>PedidoPago</code> copied to 3 queues, the same idea as Kafka's 3 consumer groups, now with queues.</li>
  <li>To run it, each folder has a <code>docker-compose.yml</code> with <code>rabbitmq:3-management</code>, on port <strong>5672</strong> (AMQP) and the dashboard on <strong>15672</strong>, with user <code>user</code> and password <code>password</code>. The dashboard shows the exchanges, the bindings and the queues filling up.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">Nine divergences between the labs and the lesson, in two groups</h3>

<p>In the code, five things that lesson 9's guarantee would call for:</p>

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The client is archived.</strong> <code>streadway/amqp</code> no longer receives changes; the replacement is <code>rabbitmq/amqp091-go</code>.</li>
  <li><strong><code>default/producer.go</code> declares the topology and publishes in a loop of 3 billion iterations</strong>, with no <em>publisher confirms</em>. It fills the queue and the broker's memory, because the producer never knows whether the broker keeps up. In 4 s, the producer printed 212,188 lines of "enviada" (sent), and the queue had 208,902 messages: without confirmation, it has no way to know how many arrived.</li>
  <li><strong>The messages are not persistent.</strong> They are <code>text/plain</code> without <code>DeliveryMode: Persistent</code>. The queue is durable, the message is transient: does it survive a broker restart? <strong>No.</strong> I tested it: with 119,161 messages in the <code>cobrar</code> queue, a <code>docker restart</code> of the broker brought back the queue and the exchange, and <strong>0</strong> messages.</li>
  <li><strong>No consumer calls <code>ch.Qos(...)</code>.</strong> Without <code>prefetch</code>, the broker pushes <strong>everything</strong> to the first consumer that connects. <code>rabbitmqctl list_channels</code> showed <code>prefetch_count = 0</code> (no limit) and 467 messages delivered and still without an <code>ack</code> at one moment of the reading.</li>
  <li><strong><code>d.Ack(true)</code> uses <code>multiple = true</code>.</strong> That acknowledges <strong>all</strong> pending messages up to that one. If processing is concurrent, messages still in progress are acknowledged and can be lost.</li>
</ol>

<p>And outside the consumers' code, four things about the repository:</p>

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The image is <code>rabbitmq:3-management</code>.</strong> The 3.13 series reached the end of community support on September 30, 2024, and the current series is 4.x.</li>
  <li><strong>The comment <code>routing key (binding key)</code> mixes the two names</strong> that lesson 9 separated.</li>
  <li><strong>Two labs use the same exchange name with different types.</strong> <code>default/</code> declares <code>ecommerce.nova.venda</code> as direct, and <code>fanout/</code> as fanout. Running both on the same broker makes the second one fail at declaration, because the type does not match. Run it: with <code>default/</code> already declared, <code>fanout/producer.go</code> prints <code>Falha ao construir a exchange Exception (406) Reason: "PRECONDITION_FAILED - inequivalent arg 'type' for exchange 'ecommerce.nova.venda' in vhost '/': received 'fanout' but current is 'direct'"</code> (the first sentence is the program's own message, in Portuguese: "Failed to build the exchange").</li>
  <li><strong>The fanout publishes 1,000 messages,</strong> not a handful: each of the 3 queues piles up all 1,000 if nobody consumes.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the duplicated email and the poison message</h3>

<p>The email consumer sends the confirmation email for 1051 and <strong>goes down before the <code>ack</code></strong>. RabbitMQ redelivers the message with <code>redelivered = true</code> and Lena receives the <strong>duplicated email</strong>. The fix is idempotence: one <code>message_id</code> per email, and the consumer ignores what it already sent. With <code>auto-ack</code>, the flaw is the opposite: the message is erased on arrival, and if the consumer goes down before sending, the email is <strong>lost</strong>.</p>

<p>The <strong>poison message</strong> (an invalid address) shows the difference between the two <code>nack</code>s. With <code>nack</code> and requeue, it comes back forever, as in lesson 3. With <code>nack</code> <strong>without</strong> requeue, it goes to the DLQ through the DLX, after 3 attempts, and the queue moves on.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>Direct, topic and fanout are three ways to route.</strong> One queue, patterns and copy to all.</li>
  <li><strong>A durable queue is not enough.</strong> Without <code>DeliveryMode: Persistent</code>, the message vanishes on a restart.</li>
  <li><strong>Without prefetch, one consumer takes everything.</strong> The broker pushes what it has to the first one that connects.</li>
  <li><strong>The ack has to be idempotent on the other side.</strong> Going down between sending and acknowledging duplicates the email.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
