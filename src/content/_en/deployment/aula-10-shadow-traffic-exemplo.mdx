<h3 class="sec">What the lab shows and what is missing</h3>

<p>What it shows: the <strong>1%</strong> copy reaches <code>chip-shadow</code> and the stable one answers as normal. That is the mechanism working. What is missing, in the order the store would feel it:</p>

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The comparator.</strong> The lab only looks at Prometheus's <code>/metrics</code>. Without joining the shadow's response with the stable one's, there is no 64.00 × 49.00 diff that lesson 9 wanted.</li>
  <li><strong>The sample.</strong> 1% of 5 orders/s is <strong>0.05/s</strong>, or <strong>3 orders per minute</strong>. For <code>pedidos</code> that is little; for <code>catalogo</code> at 640 req/s it is enough.</li>
  <li><strong>The shadow's capacity.</strong> 1 replica for a stable one of 10: the shadow has <strong>less</strong> capacity than what it copies. The math of 10% × 640 = 64/s ≤ 100/s from lesson 9 is the limit.</li>
  <li><strong>The <code>Rollout</code> with <code>canary.steps</code> on a shadow.</strong> An app that is only a shadow does not need steps; the single step (<code>setWeight: 100</code>) is noise inherited from the chart.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: mirroring 100% onto 1 replica</h3>

<p>Someone swaps <code>percent: 1</code> for <code>percent: 100</code> on <code>catalogo</code>. The mirror copies the 640 req/s to <strong>1</strong> shadow replica that handles 100: 640 ÷ 100 = <strong>640%</strong> CPU, infinite queue. Since the response is discarded, <strong>nobody sees the damage</strong> except the shadow's own metric. And if the shadow shares a database or cache with the stable one, <strong>the stable one feels it</strong>: the mirror, which should be invisible, starts taking production down.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The mirror is a line of configuration.</strong> <code>percent</code> decides the extra load; one extra zero is an incident.</li>
  <li><strong>A shadow is a separate app.</strong> Another namespace, another release, another capacity.</li>
  <li><strong>A small sample does not teach.</strong> 1% of 5/s is 3 orders per minute.</li>
  <li><strong>Damage in the shadow is silent.</strong> A discarded response does not become an error for the customer; only the shadow's metric shows it.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
