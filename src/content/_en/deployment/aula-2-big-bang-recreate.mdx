<p class="ponte">In the last lesson the way back became clear: the code comes back, the data does not, and the time depends on the model. What is left is the simplest model of all: <strong>stop everything and start again</strong>. This is the <em>Big Bang</em>, which Kubernetes calls <strong>Recreate</strong>, and it has a price you can add up in seconds.</p>

<OneLiner>With <strong>Recreate</strong> all the old pods die before any new pod is born. It is the <strong>only</strong> model where 2.4.0 and 2.5.0 never serve together, and the price is <strong>downtime</strong>.</OneLiner>

<DoIt>
  <p>In the figure, in the <strong>Recreate</strong> scenario, take down the 3 pods of 2.4.0 and follow the <strong>40 s</strong> clock until 2.5.0 serves. Notice that during that interval <code>lb-loja</code> only returns <code>503</code>.</p>
</DoIt>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The configuration.</strong> In Kubernetes it is <code>strategy.type: Recreate</code>: "all existing Pods are killed before new ones are created" (Deployment documentation). There is no <code>maxSurge</code> or <code>maxUnavailable</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>The <code>pedidos</code> math.</strong> Shutting down the 3 old pods takes 10 s; each new pod takes 30 s to become ready (the math from module 14). That is <strong>40 s</strong> serving nobody.</li>
  <li><strong>What that costs.</strong> At 5 orders/s, <strong>200</strong> orders refused in the 40 s. On the Black Friday from module 14, at 30/s, <strong>1,200</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>When it is worth it.</strong> When the two versions <strong>cannot coexist</strong> (2.5.0 changes the representation of a field that 2.4.0 does not understand), or when the gradual transition costs more than the stop.</li>
  <li><strong>The article's case: Kafka.</strong> A consumer group with <em>leasing</em> does a <em>rebalance</em> every time a pod leaves or joins. In <code>entregas</code> (3 partitions, module 13), with <strong>6 s</strong> stopped per rebalance (a declared example), rolling spends 3 swaps × 2 rebalances × 6 s = <strong>36 s</strong> with consumption interrupted in parts. Recreate does <strong>1</strong> rebalance of 6 s after the 3 new ones join.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: 2.5.0 does not start</h3>

<p>2.5.0 writes the <code>frete</code> column, and someone forgot to apply the migration. The first new pod tries to write and the database answers <code>column "frete" does not exist</code>; the pod goes into <em>CrashLoopBackOff</em>. The 3 old pods have <strong>already died</strong>: that is <strong>0 pods</strong> and 100% <code>503</code> until someone undoes it. <code>rollout undo</code> is another Recreate, with 40 s more: <strong>80 s</strong> offline in total, <strong>400</strong> orders refused at 5/s and <strong>2,400</strong> at 30/s.</p>

<p>Notice what Recreate does <strong>not</strong> do: it does not let anyone see the shipping defect beforehand, because 2.5.0 starts healthy. When the defect is a business one, Recreate is the model with the biggest radius: 100% of the traffic at once and no results gate.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>No mixed versions.</strong> It is the only guarantee only Recreate gives, and it justifies the price when versions cannot live together.</li>
  <li><strong>The price is arithmetic.</strong> 40 s × orders per second, and it doubles if the new version does not start.</li>
  <li><strong>The criterion goes beyond Kafka.</strong> What decides is the incompatibility between versions, not the type of service.</li>
  <li><strong>No gate.</strong> Recreate checks nothing before serving with the new version.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={4}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Corrections to the base article</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>"Only viable with asynchronous communication and eventual consistency."</strong> The criterion is <strong>two versions being unable to coexist</strong>, or the transition costing more than the stop. Asynchrony and eventual consistency are not prerequisites; the price is the downtime.</li>
    <li><strong>"Kafka consumers require Recreate."</strong> Cooperative <em>rebalance</em> (KIP-429) and static membership (<code>group.instance.id</code>) reduce the cost of rolling. Recreate is an option, not a requirement.</li>
  </ul>
