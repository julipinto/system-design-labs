<p class="ponte">In the last lesson the canary analysis assumed something hidden: that the <strong>data</strong> serves both versions at the same time. 2.5.0 writes <code>frete</code> in its own column, and 2.4.0 is still live during the rolling, the canary or the blue/green. The deploy goes back; <strong>the data does not</strong>. That is why the column has to be born in a way both versions can take.</p>

<OneLiner>Every production migration has to serve version <strong>N</strong> and <strong>N−1</strong>. The path is <strong>expand → deploy → <em>backfill</em> in batches → read from the new one → contract</strong>, and the migration runs <strong>before</strong> and <strong>after</strong> the deploy, never <strong>inside</strong> it.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Expand.</strong> <code>ADD COLUMN frete</code> (NULL), with <code>lock_timeout</code>. It takes 3.2 ms and 2.4.0 does not even notice: it ignores the new column.</li>
  <li><strong>Deploy 2.5.0.</strong> It writes <code>total</code> <strong>and</strong> <code>frete</code>, and reads <code>total</code>. Since 2.4.0 coexists, rolling, canary and blue/green are safe.</li>
  <li><strong>Backfill in batches.</strong> <code>frete = (detalhes-&gt;&gt;'frete')::numeric</code> on the old rows: the <code>detalhes</code> JSON from module 16 holds the shipping (1054 has <code>{'{"itens":[…],"frete":15.00}'}</code>). Batches of 10,000 rows, with a pause.</li>
  <li><strong>Deploy 2.6.0.</strong> It starts to <strong>read</strong> <code>frete</code>, behind a flag (lesson 11).</li>
  <li><strong>Contract.</strong> After the rollback window (7 days, example): <code>SET NOT NULL</code> and removal of the old code. The rule: the migration runs <strong>before</strong> (expand) and <strong>after</strong> (contract) the deploy, never <strong>inside</strong> it. Fowler calls the pattern <em>parallel change</em>.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">The batch has a price, and so does the lock</h3>

<p>The store has 50M rows in 3 shards: <strong>16.67M per shard</strong> (33 GB of data; 46.7 GB with indexes, module 16). In batches of 10,000 rows with a <strong>1 s</strong> pause that is 1,667 batches × (0.066 + 1) s ≈ <strong>30 min per shard</strong>, on all 3 in parallel (order of magnitude). The single <code>UPDATE</code> <strong>doubles</strong> the table (+33 GB per shard) and holds the rows for about 2 minutes (8 s × 16.67 ≈ 133 s).</p>

<p>And there is the <strong>lock queue</strong>. A long transaction (6 s) holds an open <code>SELECT</code>; the <code>ALTER TABLE</code> needs <code>ACCESS EXCLUSIVE</code> and <strong>waits</strong>; a simple <code>SELECT</code> that arrived <strong>after</strong> waited <strong>4.0 s</strong> behind the <code>ALTER</code>, because the queue blocks everyone. With <code>SET lock_timeout = '1s'</code> the <code>ALTER</code> gives up in <strong>1.0 s</strong> with <code>ERROR: canceling statement due to lock timeout</code> and the <code>SELECT</code> goes through. Every production migration carries <code>lock_timeout</code> and retries.</p>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: two bombs</h3>

<p><strong>The first: <code>RENAME</code> or <code>DROP</code> inside the deploy.</strong> The 90 s rolling leaves 2.4.0 reading a column that does not exist: <code>ERROR: column "total" does not exist</code>. It is the real error from the table above.</p>

<p><strong>The second is the bomb from module 16.</strong> 2.6.0 swaps <code>mod 3</code> for <code>mod 4</code> in the function that picks the shard. During the rolling 2.6.0 sees 0% → 33% → 67% → 100% of the orders (150 orders in the 90 s), and <strong>75.2%</strong> of the keys change shard: 4 of the 5 customers in the sample (<code>lena-77</code> 1 → 0, <code>bia-31</code> 1 → 3, <code>caio-58</code> 2 → 1, <code>mkt-01</code> 2 → 1; only <code>davi-12</code> stays on 0). About <strong>113</strong> of those orders go to a shard where 2.4.0 does not look.</p>

<p>And, <strong>after</strong> the rolling, 2.6.0 queries the <strong>status</strong> of Lena's orders (the endpoint the app uses by <em>short polling</em>, module 12, and that the saga and the refund also use) and <strong>does not find</strong> her 8 orders so far (1042, 1044, 1047, 1048, 1051, 1054, 1055 and 1056): they are on <code>shard-1</code>, and 2.6.0 looks on <code>shard-0</code>. The "my orders" screen (module 18) reads the projection and <strong>stays right</strong>: the error belongs to whoever reads <code>pedidos</code>.</p>

<p>The <strong>routing function is versioned data</strong> (the slot map with an epoch from module 16, lesson 8). The change follows the same expand/contract: copy with CDC, <strong>read from both</strong> during the transition, swap the map and only then delete the source.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The data does not come back.</strong> That is why the migration has to serve N and N−1.</li>
  <li><strong>Each command has a different cost.</strong> 3.2 ms for <code>ADD COLUMN</code>, 7.9 s with a volatile default, 8.0 s and +100% for the single <code>UPDATE</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>The lock blocks whoever arrives later.</strong> Without <code>lock_timeout</code>, the waiting <code>ALTER</code> makes the whole queue wait.</li>
  <li><strong>Routing is data too.</strong> Swapping <code>mod 3</code> for <code>mod 4</code> sends 113 orders to the wrong shard.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
