<p class="ponte">In the last lesson blue/green promised to test green without customers and to go back in 1 s. The author's public lab has two files that do this with <strong>Argo Rollouts</strong>: a manual one (<code>simple</code>) and one with an automatic test before the switch (<code>automated</code>). It is worth reading the lines that matter, and what they <strong>do not</strong> catch.</p>

<OneLiner>In Argo Rollouts, <code>blueGreen</code> connects two Services (<code>activeService</code> and <code>previewService</code>); the <strong>promotion</strong> swaps the pointer, and <code>prePromotionAnalysis</code> runs tests <strong>before</strong> it. It catches a <code>500</code> without customers, but it does <strong>not</strong> catch a <code>201</code> with zero shipping.</OneLiner>

<h3 class="sec">The simple one: manual promotion</h3>

<p>The author's public repository (<code>exemplos/deployment-methods/</code>) is a homelab lab: it uses Argo CD, a Helm chart, the image <code>fidelissauro/chip:v1</code> and Istio. This excerpt is the core of <code>simple</code> (read, <strong>not run</strong>; unchanged, with the line numbers of the original).</p>

<CodeLab code={bgSimple} from={37} to={45} />

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>Lines 37 and 38 fix <strong>5 replicas</strong> (<code>min: 5</code>, <code>max: 5</code>, no autoscaling).</li>
  <li>Line 42 says which Service gets the customer (<code>activeService: chip</code>) and line 43 which one gets the new version for testing (<code>previewService: chip-green</code>).</li>
  <li>Line 44, <code>autoPromotionEnabled: false</code>, makes promotion a manual act: someone looks at green and approves.</li>
  <li>Line 45, <code>scaleDownDelaySeconds: 60</code>, keeps <em>blue</em> up for <strong>60 s</strong> after the switch. The Argo default is 30 s.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">The automated one: tests before promotion</h3>

<p><code>automated</code> is the same file with <code>prePromotionAnalysis</code> (lines 46 to 49) and two analysis templates. Notice that the <code>scaleDownDelaySeconds</code> line is <strong>commented out</strong> (line 45): without it the default applies, 30 s.</p>

<CodeLab code={bgAutomated} from={41} to={49} />

<p>The first template, <code>http-bench-analysis</code>, is a Kubernetes <em>Job</em> that runs <code>cassowary</code> (a load testing tool) against green:</p>

<CodeLab code={bgAutomated} from={53} to={74} />

<p>The second, <code>chip-check-success</code>, queries Prometheus for <strong>green</strong>'s success rate:</p>

<CodeLab code={bgAutomated} from={76} to={93} />

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>Line 72 tells <code>cassowary</code> (<code>-c 3 -n 1000</code>) to make 1,000 requests, with 3 connections, to <code>http://chip-green.chip:8080/system</code>. The target is <code>chip-green</code>: green alone, with no customers.</li>
  <li>Lines 87 to 92 divide the responses that are <strong>not</strong> <code>5xx</code> by the total, only for <code>chip-green</code>. The condition on line 81, <code>result[0] >= 0.95</code>, requires 95% success.</li>
  <li>If any analysis fails, Argo does <strong>not promote</strong>: green stays aside and blue keeps serving.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">What the lab shows and what it does not</h3>

<p>The load test and the success rate run <strong>before</strong> the promotion, against green alone. That is the way to catch a <code>500</code> without any customer exposed, and it is what the lab shows well. What it does not catch:</p>

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The zero shipping.</strong> To the analysis, <code>201</code> is success. 2.5.0 passes both analyses and goes to 100% of the traffic.</li>
  <li><strong>The store's target.</strong> <code>&gt;= 0.95</code> accepts <strong>5%</strong> errors; the store's target is 0.1% (the 99.9% for checkout, module 24).</li>
  <li><strong>The sample.</strong> 1,000 requests with 3 connections are few for a rare defect, and before promotion green gets no customers: the Prometheus rate measures, in practice, the test's own traffic.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the defect shows up 8 minutes later</h3>

<p>The switch passed the tests. Eight minutes later someone sees the business metric (<code>frete_zero</code> by city, in Recife) and decides to go back. But the lab's <code>scaleDownDelaySeconds</code> is <strong>60 s</strong>, and <em>blue</em> was shut down at 60 s: going back is <strong>starting 3 pods and waiting 30 s</strong>, instead of flipping the pointer in 1 s. The "go back in 1 s" window closed long before the defect showed up.</p>

<p>The fix is to size <code>scaleDownDelaySeconds</code> by the <strong>detection time</strong>, not by the switch time. If the store only notices in 8 min, blue has to live 8 min (3 extra pods for that interval), or the canary from lesson 6 cuts the exposure from the start.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>Two pieces and a pointer.</strong> <code>activeService</code>, <code>previewService</code> and the promotion, which is the act of swapping.</li>
  <li><strong>The test before the switch catches infrastructure errors.</strong> <code>500</code>s and slowness show up without customers.</li>
  <li><strong>The limit is what you measure.</strong> <code>201</code> with zero shipping is success for the lab's analysis.</li>
  <li><strong>The delay is a cost decision.</strong> Every second of blue staying up is a paid pod; it should cover the detection time.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
