<h3 class="sec">From initial to time: who decides to advance</h3>

<p><code>initial</code> and <code>simple</code> are <strong>identical</strong>, except for <code>version</code> (<code>v3</code> and <code>v4</code>). Both have 10 replicas and these steps (read, <strong>not run</strong>; unchanged, with the line numbers of the original):</p>

<CodeLab code={canarySimple} from={37} to={53} />

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>Lines 37 and 38 fix <strong>10 replicas</strong> (<code>min: 10</code>, <code>max: 10</code>).</li>
  <li>The steps raise the weight 0 → 10 → 20 → 40 → 80 → 100, and <code>pause: {'{ }'}</code> is a pause <strong>with no end</strong>: someone promotes by hand (<code>kubectl argo rollouts promote</code>).</li>
  <li><code>time</code> swaps each pause for <code>pause: {'{duration: 10s}'}</code> (lines 44, 46, 48, 50 and 52 of the file). Promotion becomes automatic, <strong>by time, without looking at any metric</strong>: it is just a clock.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the canary by time</h3>

<p>The lab's <code>time</code>, with 10 s per step, goes 0 → 10 → 20 → 40 → 80 → 100 in <strong>5 × 10 s = 50 s</strong>. That is faster than the 3-pod rolling from lesson 3 (90 s). <strong>A canary by time without analysis is rolling with more steps</strong>: nobody looked at anything between one step and the next, and 2.5.0 with zero shipping would reach 100% before the first metric window closed.</p>

<h3 class="sec">The analysis: the success rate</h3>

<p><code>analysis</code> swaps the pauses for analyses. The steps and the analysis template, line by line:</p>

<CodeLab code={canaryAnalysis} from={41} to={73} />

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>Lines 43 to 53: 20% → 30 s pause → <strong>analysis</strong> (<code>chip-check-success</code>) → 50% → 1 min pause → analysis → 100%.</li>
  <li>Line 60: <code>interval: 2m</code>; line 73: <code>count: 1</code>. The analysis runs <strong>one</strong> measurement.</li>
  <li>Line 61: <code>successCondition: result[0] &gt;= 0.95</code>, and line 62, <code>failureLimit: 1</code>.</li>
  <li>Lines 66 to 72: the query divides the <code>irate</code> of the responses that are <strong>not</strong> <code>5xx</code> by the <code>irate</code> of all of them, with a 5 minute window.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">Five things the lab leaves out</h3>

<p>The lab is a minimal example, and the five below are what separates an example from an analysis for the store.</p>

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>5% tolerance.</strong> <code>&gt;= 0.95</code> accepts 5% of <code>5xx</code>. The store's target is <strong>99.9%</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>It only sees <code>5xx</code>.</strong> <code>response_code!~"5.*"</code> counts the <code>201</code> with shipping 0 as success. Only a <strong>business metric</strong> catches it.</li>
  <li><strong>Whole service.</strong> <code>destination_service=~"chip.chip.svc.cluster.local"</code> measures the whole service (stable + canary) and <strong>dilutes</strong> the canary's error by its weight. To measure only the canary you have to filter by subset or by version.</li>
  <li><strong><code>irate</code> in a 5 m window.</strong> The Prometheus documentation says to use <code>rate</code> for rules and alerts and <code>irate</code> only for graphs of volatile counters.</li>
  <li><strong><code>count: 1</code>.</strong> It is <strong>one</strong> measurement: the <code>interval: 2m</code> does not even matter.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">The store's analysis (an adaptation, not run)</h3>

<p>The query the store would use swaps the response code for the business defect, by version and by city, and requires zero:</p>

<pre><code>{`sum(increase(pedidos_criados_total{versao="2.5.0",frete="0"}[5m])) by (cidade)

successCondition: result[0] == 0`}</code></pre>

<p>It is an <strong>adaptation</strong> (it was not run, no cluster). With <code>by (cidade)</code> the query returns one series per city, so the real Argo condition needs adjusting (sum everything, or check each series). The idea is what matters: at the 10% step, the Recife series shows 30 and the canary aborts, with R$ 450 of loss instead of R$ 54,000.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>A pause with no end is a human decision.</strong> <code>pause: {'{ }'}</code> waits for someone to promote; <code>duration</code> is just a clock.</li>
  <li><strong>An analysis is a criterion, and the criterion is in the query.</strong> <code>&gt;= 0.95</code> over <code>5xx</code> is permissive and blind to the business.</li>
  <li><strong>What you measure decides the radius.</strong> The whole service dilutes the canary; only the canary shows the canary.</li>
  <li><strong>One measurement is not a trend.</strong> <code>count: 1</code> decides with one point.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
