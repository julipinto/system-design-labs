<p class="ponte">In the last lesson <code>celula-mkt</code> went down on its own and Lena closed 1060 in <code>celula-recife</code>. What's left is opening the box: what is <strong>inside</strong> a cell, what it costs and what happens to a cat that exists on only one shelf.</p>

<OneLiner>A cell isolates along <strong>five dimensions at once</strong> (execution, persistence, observability, deploy and failure), and <strong>all outside communication goes through its gateway</strong>. Isolating only the database is a <em>shard</em>, and only the CPU is a <em>bulkhead</em>.</OneLiner>

<h3 class="sec">The five dimensions</h3>

<p>The path above shows <strong>execution</strong> and <strong>persistence</strong> inside the wall. The other three only show up when something goes wrong, and a cell is only a cell if all five hold:</p>

<table class="sr-table">
  <thead><tr><th>Dimension</th><th>What is isolated</th><th>In <code>celula-recife</code></th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><th>Execution</th><td>CPU, memory, replicas, autoscaling</td><td>its own pods, with a floor of 2 per service</td></tr>
    <tr><th>Persistence</th><td>database, cache, queue</td><td>PostgreSQL, Redis and the <code>pedidos.recife</code> topic</td></tr>

<p>Two <strong>global</strong> components break the design: a shared queue or a single cache. If one has to exist, it comes in through a cell component at the edge (lesson 3). Inside, the opposite rule holds: each service in the cell only knows the ones in its own cell.</p>

<h3 class="sec">State: passive replication only</h3>

<p>The cell <strong>shares no state</strong> with the others, except for a <strong>passive</strong> copy of its database in another cell, which only takes over if the active one goes down (lesson 4). That is where the freedom the article mentions comes from: each cell can have its own <em>tuning</em>, <em>runtime</em> version or experiment with no risk to the others.</p>

<h3 class="sec">How big is a cell: sizing</h3>

<p>AWS asks three things of the size. The cell has to <strong>fit the biggest load</strong> one customer can bring, be <strong>testable at real scale</strong> and still have <strong>economies of scale</strong> (if it is too small, the floor eats everything). AWS also recommends a <strong>maximum limit</strong> per cell and cells of the <strong>same size</strong>.</p>

<p>The store <strong>does not follow</strong> the last recommendation: <code>celula-mkt</code> has 40% and <code>celula-recife</code> has 12%. The registry map decides who goes where, and <code>mkt-01</code> got its own cell because it was the noisy neighbor. The price shows up in lesson 4: unequal cells make the <em>failover</em> headroom unequal.</p>

<h3 class="sec">The cost of the floor</h3>

<p>A cell <strong>does not shrink to zero</strong>: each service needs at least <strong>2 replicas</strong> to survive one pod. With the 2027 plan from module 23 (200 orders/s and 950 req/s on the catalog; 1 replica = 1 vCPU; target ρ of 70%) and the four services in the math, the total changes like this:</p>

<p>That is <strong>+12 vCPU (+29.3%)</strong>. <code>estoque</code> goes from 2 to <strong>8</strong> (the floor, four times), <code>pedidos</code> and <code>pagamentos</code> pay the rounding <strong>per cell</strong>, and the catalog goes up because the <code>mkt-01</code> cap (100 req/s, module 25) reserves 2 replicas.</p>

<p>On a <strong>regular day</strong> (5 orders/s) the single stack uses <strong>9 vCPU</strong> (2 + 3 + 2 + 2) and the four cells, at the floor, use <strong>4 × 8 = 32 vCPU</strong>: <strong>3.6×</strong>. That is the price of isolation, and it is the loss of <strong>statistical multiplexing</strong> from module 25 (Erlang C): one larger fleet serves the same load better than four smaller ones.</p>

<h3 class="sec">Stock per cell: the last cat</h3>

<p>The <code>estoque</code> is <strong>also the cell's</strong>, and a cat is a physical object on a shelf (module 6: <code>GATO-042</code>). The 19 units left after 1056 are split <strong>in proportion to demand</strong>: 33 · 15 · 12 · 40% of 19 give 6.3 · 2.9 · 2.3 · 7.6. Rounded, <code>celula-sp</code> <strong>6</strong>, <code>celula-rio</code> <strong>3</strong>, <code>celula-recife</code> <strong>2</strong> and <code>celula-mkt</code> <strong>8</strong> (sum 19). The three individual-customer cells have one warehouse each: <code>SP-1</code>, <code>RIO-1</code> and <code>REC-1</code>.</p>

<p>Order 1060 takes the 2 cats from <code>celula-recife</code>, which is left with <strong>0</strong>. The other three add up to <strong>17</strong>. The next Recife customer sees "sold out" <strong>with 17 cats in the network</strong>.</p>

<h3 class="sec">Deploy per cell</h3>

<p>Module 22 split the version by <em>shard</em> rings (20 · 20 · 60%). Now the rings are <strong>cells</strong>, and each has a name and a version (<code>celula-recife@2.6.0</code>). The cumulative exposure goes like this:</p>

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Cell zero</strong> (synthetic): test customers <code>sint-01…03</code> with ZIP codes from SP, Rio and Recife. <strong>0%</strong> of real customers.</li>
  <li><strong><code>celula-recife</code>:</strong> <strong>12%</strong> of the traffic.</li>
  <li><strong><code>celula-rio</code>:</strong> cumulative <strong>27%</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong><code>celula-sp</code>:</strong> cumulative <strong>60%</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong><code>celula-mkt</code>:</strong> <strong>100%</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<p>There is a trap. The regional cell is <strong>not representative</strong> of the regional defect. Version 2.5.0 from module 22 (shipping 0.00 in Recife) would break <strong>100% of the Recife customers</strong> in the very first real ring, even though they are 12% of the traffic. What protects you is <strong>cell zero with a Recife ZIP code</strong>: it finds the defect with <code>frete_zero</code> = 1 synthetic order and a loss of <strong>R$ 0</strong>.</p>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the last cat</h3>

<p><code>celula-recife</code> zeroed its stock and the network has 17 cats. The three ways out cost different things:</p>

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Accept the "local sold out".</strong> Loses the sale and stays consistent. For a cat at 79.90, that is expensive.</li>
  <li><strong>Rebalance.</strong> The control plane moves 2 units from SP to Recife, <strong>asynchronously</strong>, within seconds, when the allocation drops below 20%. This is the path taken.</li>
  <li><strong>Reserve across cells</strong> with a <em>saga</em> (module 19). It sells, but <strong>couples</strong> two cells on the critical path, which is what the cell exists to avoid.</li>
</ol>

<p>The temptation is a <strong>global</strong> <code>estoque</code>. The <em>oversell</em> goes away, but the radius is back to <strong>100%</strong>, the same hidden channel as in lesson 1.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>Five dimensions together.</strong> Execution, persistence, observability, deploy and failure: if one is missing, the design becomes a <em>shard</em> or a <em>bulkhead</em>.</li>
  <li><strong>The floor is expensive.</strong> 41 → 53 vCPU in the 2027 plan and 9 → 32 on a regular day.</li>
  <li><strong>A physical resource does not follow the key.</strong> The cat is allocated per cell, and the last one leaves a cell sold out with 17 in the network.</li>
  <li><strong>Deploy is the main risk.</strong> Cell zero finds the defect before any real customer.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={4}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Corrections to the base article and what it lacks</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Size, placement and migration.</strong> The article says the segmentation is deterministic and that "tomorrow the customer is still in cell X". That holds for <strong>reading</strong> the map. The <strong>control plane</strong> changes the map (placing and migrating customers when a cell fills up, lesson 3). The article lacks cell size (a maximum limit and cells of the same size, in AWS), placement and migration.</li>
    <li><strong>Cost.</strong> The article does not mention the floor. The math (41 → 53 vCPU; 9 → 32) is done here with the module 23 plan.</li>
    <li><strong>Stock.</strong> A <strong>global</strong> physical resource does not follow the "customer" key. AWS treats this as the service's <strong>grain</strong>: allocating per cell is a decision, not a detail.</li>
    <li><strong>Global dependencies.</strong> DNS, identity (the JWT), the PSP, the certificates and the map itself remain shared. The article does not list them.</li>
    <li><strong>Deploy.</strong> The article mentions "complex pipelines" and experimentation in cells. The 0 → 12 → 27 → 60 → 100% rings and cell zero are the design that module 22 calls for.</li>
  </ul>
