<h3 class="sec">Step 6: cost and margins</h3>

<p>A replica is 1 vCPU. Black Friday 2026 used catalog 10 + <code>pedidos</code> 3 + <code>pagamentos</code> 2 + <code>estoque</code> 2 = <strong>17 vCPU</strong>. The 2027 plan asks for catalog <strong>14</strong> (or 19, with search) + <code>pedidos</code> <strong>10</strong> + <code>pagamentos</code> <strong>15</strong> + <code>estoque</code> <strong>2</strong> = <strong>41 vCPU</strong>, that is, <strong>+24</strong>. The ordinary day is 2 + 3 + 2 + 2 = <strong>9</strong>.</p>

<p>In <strong>replica-hours per day</strong>, a fixed fleet of 24 h × 41 gives <strong>984</strong>. An <strong>elastic</strong> fleet, with 41 during the 4 peak hours and 9 the rest of the time, gives 4 × 41 + 20 × 9 = <strong>344</strong>: <strong>35%</strong> of the fixed one, in the same range as module 14's 33%.</p>

<p>The HPA reacts in about 60 s, and the 20:00 newsletter is <strong>known</strong>. So the plan says to <strong>pre-scale at 19:50</strong> (scheduled scaling) instead of waiting for the metric. The module 21 <strong>emergency capacity</strong> also enters the math: the catalog's degradation levels (7.5 ms → 133 req/s per replica; 6 ms → 167) give 14 × 133 = <strong>1,867</strong> and 14 × 167 = <strong>2,333 req/s</strong> without one extra replica. The plan <strong>counts</strong> this slack but <strong>doesn't depend on it</strong>: degrading is the last resort.</p>

<p>The <strong>re-evaluation trigger</strong> is: demand above <strong>210 orders/s</strong> or <strong>980 req/s</strong>, or <code>pagamentos</code> with L &gt; 4. Once it's passed, the math is redone. Step 8: the plan is a model, and the validation is <strong>measuring</strong> (module 24).</p>

<p>And there is a <strong>freeze</strong>: code changes (module 22) stop <strong>3 days</strong> before Black Friday, except for operation flags.</p>

<h3 class="sec">If it goes wrong: the plan is nominal</h3>

<p>The plan says <strong>300/s</strong> and the math is of <strong>nominal capacity</strong>: each pod's CPU, the DB per primary. Nobody measured the connection pool (<code>max_connections</code>), the Kafka queue, the PSP limit or the difference between the ordinary day's mix and Black Friday's. A plan without a test is a <strong>hypothesis</strong>, and the <em>breakpoint</em> test may find the limit <strong>before</strong> 300.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The sheet is the deliverable.</strong> Sustainable, goals, pool and triggers: sales and the on-call read the same paper.</li>
  <li><strong>Cost is part of the plan.</strong> 41 vCPU at the peak and 344 replica-hours per day with the elastic fleet.</li>
  <li><strong>The plan needs a test.</strong> Every number was calculated, and module 24 says which of them hold up.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
