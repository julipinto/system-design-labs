<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The series.</strong> The catalog peak over the last 4 years (example): <strong>2023: 300 · 2024: 410 · 2025: 520 · 2026: 640</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>Linear.</strong> The least-squares line grows <strong>113 per year</strong> and gives <strong>750</strong> in 2027.</li>
  <li><strong>Nonlinear (compound).</strong> The rate is (640 ÷ 300)^(1/3) = <strong>28.7% per year</strong> and the extrapolation gives <strong>824</strong>. The real jumps (1.37 · 1.27 · 1.23) are <strong>falling</strong>, and even so the compound lands at 824.</li>
  <li><strong>By business event.</strong> The module 9 new home screen makes <strong>15% more requests</strong> to the catalog per visit (example): 824 × 1.15 = 947.5 ≈ <strong>950</strong>, the module 14 number.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">The range and the checkout</h3>

<p>The planning range is <strong>750 – 950 – 1,140</strong>, where 1,140 = 950 × 1.2 is the error margin. For the checkout, the math is different. Today it is <strong>30 orders/s</strong>. The individual-customer trend gives 30 × 1.484 (950 ÷ 640) = <strong>44.5 ≈ 45/s</strong>. The <strong>sales goal is 200/s</strong>, and the difference of <strong>155/s</strong> only exists if <strong>partners through the API</strong> sell without browsing the catalog.</p>

<p>So 200 ÷ 950 = <strong>21%</strong> orders per catalog request, against 30 ÷ 640 = <strong>4.7%</strong> today. The planner <strong>asks the business</strong> where the 155/s come from, and the answer is the partner API. Capacity planning doesn't predict: it calculates what it costs to carry each scenario.</p>

<h3 class="sec">If it goes wrong: the line and the event</h3>

<p>The plan uses the <strong>line</strong> (750) and the event arrives (950). The 10 replicas sit at <strong>95%</strong> and the p95 goes back to 800 ms (module 14). Worse than that is <strong>unplanned nonlinear</strong> growth, like the 200 orders/s goal: it is the most dangerous kind for a system operating near its maximum.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>History is a floor, not the plan.</strong> The line and the compound say what happens if nothing changes.</li>
  <li><strong>The event has a date and an owner.</strong> The new screen, the partner API and the campaign go into the plan with a date.</li>
  <li><strong>The range shows the risk.</strong> 750 to 1,140 makes clear how much each choice of replicas covers.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={3}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">What the base article leaves out</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>The cost of the goal.</strong> The distance between the trend (45/s) and the goal (200/s) is a business decision, not an extrapolation. The article treats projection as organic growth only.</li>
    <li><strong>The rates are falling.</strong> The jumps of 1.37 · 1.27 · 1.23 show a curve that is slowing down, and the compound (28.7%) is optimistic for the history alone. That is why the line and the compound both appear.</li>
  </ul>
