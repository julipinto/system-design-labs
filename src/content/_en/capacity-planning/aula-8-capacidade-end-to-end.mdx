<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Individual.</strong> Each link has its own limit: <code>pedidos</code> 90/s, <code>pagamentos</code> 40/s, <code>estoque</code> 1,000/s, DB 250/s effective.</li>
  <li><strong>Systemic.</strong> The minimum of the chain: <strong>40/s</strong>, <code>pagamentos</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>Fix one link.</strong> Raising <code>pagamentos</code> makes the minimum move to the next weakest link.</li>
  <li><strong>Repeat.</strong> The sequence ends when the minimum reaches the demand, or when the next link costs more than it is worth.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<p>The numbers are <strong>nominal capacity</strong>. The <strong>sustainable</strong> one is 70% of it: 70% of 300 = <strong>210/s</strong> against the goal of <strong>200/s</strong>. The plan fits with about <strong>5%</strong> of slack. In the stress scenario (300/s) the chain goes to 100%.</p>

<h3 class="sec">The database and the hot shard</h3>

<p>With <code>mkt-01</code> (60% of the traffic on <code>shard-2</code>), 200 × 0.6 = <strong>120/s on a 150 shard</strong>: 80%, at the knee. The other two shards sit at 40/s (26.7%). With the <strong>dedicated 2× shard</strong> (300/s capacity) for <code>mkt-01</code> (40% = 80/s), it sits at <strong>26.7%</strong>, and the three normal ones, with (200 − 80) ÷ 3 = 40/s each, also at <strong>26.7%</strong>. It is the T = 750 math from module 16.</p>

<h3 class="sec">The asynchronous link: <code>entregas</code></h3>

<p>The Kafka <code>pedidos</code> topic (module 13) has 3 partitions and each <code>entregas</code> consumer processes <strong>40 events/s</strong> (example): <strong>120/s</strong> against <strong>200/s</strong> arriving. The lag grows <strong>80/s</strong>. In 4 hours, the event's, that is <strong>1,152,000</strong> delayed events. After the peak (5/s), at 115/s net, it takes <strong>10,017 s ≈ 2.8 h</strong> to drain. It doesn't take down the checkout, but it delays tracking.</p>

<p>The plan takes the topic from <strong>3 to 8 partitions</strong>: 8 × 40 = 320/s, ρ = <strong>62.5%</strong>. Careful with module 16: changing the number of partitions changes <code>murmur2 mod N</code>, and going from 3 to 6 moves about 50% of the keys.</p>

<h3 class="sec">If it goes wrong: scaling the most expensive link</h3>

<p>The team scales <code>estoque</code> (from 2 to 4 pods) because "stock is critical". Its capacity goes to 2,000/s and the <strong>chain's output doesn't change</strong>: 40/s. The bottleneck is the <strong>minimum</strong>, not the most expensive or the most important.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The chain is worth its weakest link.</strong> Scaling another link spends money without moving the output.</li>
  <li><strong>The bottleneck changes place.</strong> Each step of the plan finds the next one.</li>
  <li><strong>Sustainable is not nominal.</strong> The 200/s goal has to fit in 70% of the capacity, and it does, with 5% slack.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={3}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Correction to the base article</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Nominal × operational capacity.</strong> The article calculates capacity from each component's throughput. That is the <strong>nominal</strong> capacity (each pod's CPU, DB per primary). Connection pool, Kafka queue and PSP limit also have a ceiling and stay out of the math; module 24 measures the operational capacity.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Percentiles don't add up</h4>
  <p>To look at the latency of the whole chain, you can't sum or average the links' percentiles: each link's p99 doesn't give the p99 of the whole. The right aggregation is through histograms (Prometheus).</p>
