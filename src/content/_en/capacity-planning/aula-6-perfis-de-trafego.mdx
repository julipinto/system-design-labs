<p class="ponte">Last lesson showed that "640 req/s" hides the mix and the burst. The third thing the average hides is still missing: <strong>when</strong> the load arrives. The catalog has 40 req/s on average on an ordinary day, and the number that matters for the plan is the one from the <strong>worst minute</strong>.</p>

<OneLiner>The <strong>daily, weekly and seasonal</strong> profile shows when the system gets tight and when it sits idle. What gets planned is the <strong>peak of the window that matters</strong>, not the monthly average.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>The day.</strong> The catalog series on an ordinary day (example, average 40 req/s) has a minimum of <strong>4.1</strong> at 2 am, lunch between 12 pm and 1 pm and a <strong>peak of 81.5 at 9 pm</strong>: 2.04 × the average.</li>
  <li><strong>The week.</strong> Factor over the weekly average: Mon 1.05 · Tue 1.00 · Wed 1.00 · Thu 1.05 · <strong>Fri 1.15</strong> · Sat 0.90 · Sun 0.85.</li>
  <li><strong>The year.</strong> Month index: Jan 0.85 · Feb 0.80 · Mar 0.90 · Apr 0.90 · May 1.00 · Jun 0.95 · Jul 0.90 · Aug 0.95 · Sep 1.00 · Oct 1.10 · <strong>Nov 1.45</strong> · Dec 1.20. November has 1.45 × 40 = <strong>58 req/s</strong> on average.</li>
  <li><strong>The window.</strong> The larger the aggregation window, the smaller the peak: 1 to 10 minutes for the day, 1 to 5 hours for the week, days for the year.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">The day that breaks the pattern</h3>

<p>Black Friday doesn't follow the profile. The <strong>day average</strong> was <strong>400 req/s</strong> (10 times the annual average) and the <strong>20:03 peak</strong>, <strong>640</strong>. It is the newsletter minute, which the store <strong>knows</strong> (20:00). The math: 640 ÷ 81.5 = <strong>7.9 times</strong> the peak of an ordinary day.</p>

<p>In module 21 the same night shows up with <strong>1,300 req/s at 20:20</strong>. It isn't another peak: it is a <strong>burst of seconds</strong>. In the 1-minute window the same night shows up as about 650. Aggregation hides the burst, which is why the chosen window is part of the plan.</p>

<h3 class="sec">If it goes wrong: planning by the month or by the ordinary peak</h3>

<p>The plan uses the <strong>monthly index</strong> (November, 58 req/s) or the peak of an ordinary day (81.5). Black Friday is <strong>11 times</strong> the month's average and <strong>7.9 times</strong> the ordinary peak. The month's average <strong>hides the day</strong> and the day <strong>hides the minute</strong>. What gets planned is the peak of the window that matters: the 20:03 minute.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The average changes with the window.</strong> A minute, a day and a month give different numbers for the same store.</li>
  <li><strong>Known events go into the plan.</strong> The 20:00 newsletter has a set time, so it can be pre-scaled (lesson 9).</li>
  <li><strong>The profile says when capacity is spare.</strong> On an ordinary day the catalog uses 4 req/s at 2 am, and the elastic fleet takes advantage of that.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
