<p class="ponte">With the knee at 80–85% and the target at 70%, the plan already knows <strong>where to stop</strong>. To know how much load fits before that, the load has to be measured for real. "640 req/s" is a single number, and many different loads fit inside it.</p>

<OneLiner>Load has <strong>three dimensions</strong>: <strong>TPS</strong>, <strong>concurrency</strong> and <strong>payload</strong>. The <strong>average</strong> hides the request <strong>mix</strong>, the <strong>burst</strong> inside the second and the payload <strong>tail</strong>.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>TPS.</strong> Work divided by seconds. The 2026 peak was 640 req/s: 38,400 requests in 60 s.</li>
  <li><strong>Concurrency.</strong> By Little's Law, L = 640 × 0.0378 s = <strong>24.2</strong> in flight on the healthy replicas (in module 14, 86.4 across the fleet with the hot replica).</li>
  <li><strong>Payload.</strong> <code>GET /produtos/42</code> carries <strong>1 KB</strong> of request and <strong>19 KB</strong> of response (example): <strong>20 KB</strong> per transaction.</li>
  <li><strong>Bandwidth.</strong> <strong>TPS × payload</strong>: 640 × 20 KB = <strong>13.1 MB/s = 105 Mbit/s</strong>. With 950 req/s it rises to 19.5 MB/s (<strong>156 Mbit/s</strong>) and with 10% retries to <strong>171 Mbit/s</strong>.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">What the average hides (1): the mix</h3>

<p>Not every request costs the same CPU. The page costs <strong>10 ms</strong>; the heavy search (module 7), <strong>80 ms</strong> (example). With <strong>5%</strong> searches, the average CPU is 0.95 × 10 + 0.05 × 80 = <strong>13.5 ms</strong> and each replica serves <strong>74.1 req/s</strong>, not 100. For 950 req/s at 70%, the math is ⌈ 950 ÷ (0.7 × 74.1) ⌉ = <strong>19 replicas</strong>, not 14. With 10% searches, the average CPU goes to 17 ms, to 58.8 req/s per replica and to <strong>24 replicas</strong>. Capacity is measured <strong>per request type</strong> (module 24).</p>

<h3 class="sec">What the average hides (2): the burst inside the second</h3>

<p>The 640 req/s <strong>spread</strong> across the second leave each replica at 64% (R = 37.8 ms). The <strong>same</strong> 640 arriving in a <strong>10 ms</strong> burst, like the 20:00 newsletter, are <strong>64 per replica at once</strong>. The replica serves 1 every 10 ms (100/s), and the last one in line waits 64 × 10 ms = <strong>640 ms</strong> (the average, about 320 ms). The average TPS is the same, and the experience is different.</p>

<h3 class="sec">What the average hides (3): the payload spread</h3>

<p>A 19 KB <code>GET</code> and a 2 MB one (the photo without a CDN, module 5) are the "same TPS" with 100 times the bandwidth. The risk is <strong>in the tail</strong>, not in the average.</p>

<p>For the whole system, the math is the article's: <strong>systemic TPS = min (app TPS, DB TPS, cache TPS, …)</strong>. Lesson 8 applies this math to the checkout chain.</p>

<h3 class="sec">If it goes wrong: "100 req/s per replica" with search in the traffic</h3>

<p>The team plans with the page's 100 req/s and ignores that 5% of the traffic is search. For 950 req/s it computes <strong>14 replicas</strong> where <strong>19</strong> were needed. The fleet starts at <strong>91.6%</strong> CPU (950 ÷ (14 × 74.1)), already above the safe range, and passes 95% on the day search is on sale.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>TPS alone doesn't size anything.</strong> The mix changes the CPU per request, and with it the number of replicas.</li>
  <li><strong>The burst lives inside the second.</strong> The per-second average doesn't show the 640 ms queue.</li>
  <li><strong>The payload decides the bandwidth.</strong> And the payload tail decides when the bandwidth runs out.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={3}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Correction to the base article</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>"No metric is more important than TPS."</strong> The article makes TPS the central metric. TPS alone doesn't size anything: the <strong>mix</strong> and the <strong>burst</strong> dominate the math, as the lesson shows.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4 class="deep-h">The estimation equations, with the store</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>TPS</strong> = work ÷ seconds (38,400 ÷ 60 = 640).</li>
    <li><strong>Concurrency</strong> = TPS × response time (640 × 0.0378 = 24.2).</li>
    <li><strong>Bytes per transaction</strong> = request + response (1 + 19 = 20 KB).</li>
    <li><strong>Bandwidth</strong> = TPS × bytes per transaction (640 × 20 KB = 105 Mbit/s).</li>
  </ul>
