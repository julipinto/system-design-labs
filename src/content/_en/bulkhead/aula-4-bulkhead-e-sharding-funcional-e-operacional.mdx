<p class="ponte">Last lesson the radius stayed at 60% because of one customer and at 100% because of a service everyone shares. Both holes have the same origin: we split <strong>something</strong> without saying <strong>by which criterion</strong>. Module 15 gave the two axes of the cube, and they become two kinds of bulkhead: one cuts by what the system <strong>does</strong> and the other by <strong>how much and whose</strong>.</p>

<OneLiner>The <strong>functional</strong> bulkhead splits by <strong>function</strong> (Y axis): order, report, delivery. The <strong>operational</strong> one splits by <strong>volume or customer</strong> (Z axis): shards, region. Both only truly isolate if they separate <strong>every</strong> resource on the path.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Functional.</strong> The store has three functions with opposite profiles. <code>pedidos</code> is transactional (p95 ≤ 800 ms). <code>relatorios</code> is analytical: the 23:00 report from module 6, which now <strong>reads from the Rio replica</strong> (module 17) and uses <strong>0%</strong> of the primary's write capacity. <code>entregas</code> is asynchronous, on Kafka. Each one has its own resources.</li>
  <li><strong>Operational.</strong> The split is by <strong>how much and whose</strong>. The 3 <code>pedidos</code> shards are by <code>cliente_id</code> (module 16), <code>mkt-01</code> gets a <strong>dedicated shard</strong>, and the region is also an operational cut.</li>
  <li><strong>The topic case.</strong> The newsletter of <strong>50,000 emails</strong> (module 13) already goes through RabbitMQ, separate from the Kafka <code>pedidos</code> topic. If it were on the <strong>same</strong> topic, the 3 <code>entregas</code> consumers, at <strong>40 events/s</strong> each (<strong>120/s</strong>, module 23), would take 50,000 ÷ 120 = <strong>417 s ≈ 7 min</strong> to get to the order events. Separated, the order delay is <strong>0</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>Priority.</strong> The same load, three queues: 20 workers split into <strong>high 12</strong> (customer checkout), <strong>normal 6</strong> (refunds and partners) and <strong>low 2</strong> (reprocessing). A burst of <code>low</code>, like the 500/s bot from module 8 or the reconciliation, <strong>never goes past 2 workers</strong>. Checkout keeps its 12.</li>
  <li><strong>The whole path.</strong> Splitting only the database is not enough. The flow has to stay on the same slice of the <strong>load balancer</strong>, the <strong>application</strong>, the <strong>database</strong> and the <strong>queue</strong>. A single common resource in the middle opens a channel from one end to the other.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<p>The split by function is the one that already came from module 15, and the bulkhead gives it the reason it lacked: the report left the primary not just for tidiness, but so the checkout 503 <strong>does not happen</strong>. The operational split brings back the math from lesson 3: the <code>mkt-01</code> shard's radius is the worst case, and the dedicated one reduces it from 60% to 40%.</p>

<h3 class="sec">And if it goes wrong: the "isolated" shard that shares the inventory</h3>

<p>It is common for shards to start out well isolated and, little by little, begin sharing <strong>global dependencies</strong>: a configuration service, a catalog, an auxiliary database. The dedicated <code>mkt-01</code> shard has its own database, but it keeps calling the <strong>same</strong> <code>estoque</code> and reading the <strong>same global configuration</strong>. A wrong configuration, or an <code>estoque</code> saturated by the bot, hits all 4 shards at once. The article calls this a <strong>hidden channel</strong>. Without separating the whole path, the design has 4 boxes and a single fate.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>Functional separates what the system does.</strong> The report goes to the Rio replica, and the primary is left with 0% heavy queries.</li>
  <li><strong>Operational separates by customer and volume.</strong> The dedicated shard takes the worst case from 60% to 40%.</li>
  <li><strong>The same topic is the same compartment.</strong> The newsletter on the orders topic delays events by 417 s; on RabbitMQ, by 0.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={4}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Corrections to the source article</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>"Deterministic" consistent hashing.</strong> The hash <strong>routes</strong>; the isolation comes from the resources being <strong>separate</strong> per shard. And when N changes (module 16), <strong>25%</strong> of keys migrate and the radius changes with them.</li>
    <li><strong>Segregate everything, end to end.</strong> The article says this in the sharding section, after calling each shard a "complete or partial bulkhead". Here the difference is the rule: the shard is partial until it separates load balancer, application, database, topic and queue.</li>
    <li><strong>The report on the replica is physical.</strong> Taking the report off the primary is a functional split that becomes physical: another machine, another disk. The 300 ms lag (module 17) does not matter to it.</li>
  </ul>
