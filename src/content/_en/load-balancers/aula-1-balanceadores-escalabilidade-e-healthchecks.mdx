<p>Module 6 ended with the store split into services and <code>catalogo</code> running on several replicas for Black Friday. But Lena types <code>loja.com.br</code> and DNS (module 1) returns <strong>one</strong> IP. Which replica answers? With nobody in the middle, everyone lands on the same one, <code>catalogo-1</code>, which saturates while the others sit idle. It's the grocery store with a single open register and the other cashiers watching.</p>

<OneLiner>A load balancer sits in front of the replicas, takes every request on a single address and picks, request by request, who answers; a healthcheck removes from the pool the replica that stopped responding.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>No load balancer.</strong> The only address DNS knows is <code>catalogo-1</code>'s (<code>10.0.1.11</code>). The first 9 requests all go to it: CPU at <strong>100%</strong>, while <code>catalogo-2</code> (<code>10.0.1.12</code>) and <code>catalogo-3</code> (<code>10.0.1.13</code>) stay at <strong>0%</strong>. The cat page, which took <strong>20 ms</strong>, now takes <strong>1.8 s</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>With a load balancer.</strong> DNS now returns the IP of <code>lb-loja</code>, <code>203.0.113.10</code> (a documentation range, just an example). <code>lb-loja</code> keeps a list of replicas, the <strong>pool</strong>, and forwards each request to one of them, on port <code>8080</code>. Here the pool has only three replicas to fit on the screen; on module 6's Black Friday there were 10. Lena never sees the replicas, only the balancer.</li>
  <li><strong>Healthcheck.</strong> To avoid sending requests to a replica that doesn't answer, the balancer runs <code>GET /health</code> on each replica every <strong>5 s</strong>, with a <strong>1 s timeout</strong>. Three failures in a row take the replica out of the pool. Two successes in a row put it back.</li>
  <li><strong>When a replica hangs.</strong> At 20:00:00 <code>catalogo-2</code> hangs. The checks at 20:00:05, 20:00:10 and 20:00:15 fail, and it leaves the pool at <strong>20:00:15</strong>. During those <strong>15 s</strong>, <strong>1 in every 3</strong> requests lands on it and comes back with an error (502 or 504). After that, 100% of requests go to replicas 1 and 3.</li>
  <li><strong>When it comes back, and when the store grows.</strong> <code>catalogo-2</code> returns at 20:05:00. The checks at 20:05:05 and 20:05:10 pass, and at <strong>20:05:10</strong> it is back in the pool. To scale, <code>catalogo-4</code> (<code>10.0.1.14</code>) joins: as soon as its first healthcheck passes, the balancer already sends it requests, with nobody telling Lena.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>Scale without changing the address.</strong> Adding <code>catalogo-4</code> changes nothing for those outside, who keep talking to <code>203.0.113.10</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>Failure becomes degradation.</strong> With 3 replicas, one hung replica drops a third of the requests for 15 s, not the whole store.</li>
  <li><strong>Maintenance without a scare.</strong> Taking a replica out of the pool to update it is the same move the healthcheck makes on its own when it breaks.</li>
  <li><strong>The price.</strong> The balancer becomes the new single point of failure. That's why, in production, it is duplicated too.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={5}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">The 15 s window is a choice</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Time to leave:</strong> interval × threshold (5 s × 3 = 15 s). If the replica hangs instead of refusing the connection, the last check only fails at the end of the timeout (1 s more).</li>
    <li><strong>A shorter interval</strong> finds the problem sooner and creates more check traffic. <strong>A higher threshold</strong> avoids removing a replica for a 1 s hiccup, and takes longer to react.</li>
    <li><strong>Returning takes less than leaving</strong> (2 against 3) on purpose: a replica that just came back is usually fragile, and the asymmetry keeps it from flapping in and out of the pool.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Active and passive healthchecks</h4>
  <p>What the store does here is the <strong>active</strong> kind: the balancer asks. The <strong>passive</strong> kind watches real requests and removes the replica that is failing (in Envoy it's called outlier detection). In Envoy's documentation, without an active healthcheck configured on the cluster, all members count as healthy.</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">What the source article leaves out</h4>
  <ul>
    <li>The article says the balancer offers "mechanisms to constantly check the health of the hosts", with no numbers. The 5 s, 1 s, 3 and 2 in this lesson are the store's own, as an example, and are not the default values of any tool.</li>
  </ul>
