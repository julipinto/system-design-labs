<p>The store keeps 500,000 photos like gato.jpg, and a folder tree with that many photos gets heavy. Object storage removes the tree: each photo has a key, and the server asks for the photo by it.</p>

<OneLiner>object storage keeps each thing as an object (content, metadata and a unique key) in a bucket, and you access it through an API, with no real folders.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>The store creates a <strong>bucket</strong> (a "pail") with a unique name: <code>loja-fotos</code>.</li>
  <li>gato.jpg becomes an <strong>object</strong>: the 2,457,600 bytes, plus metadata (<code>image/jpeg</code>, <code>produto=4521</code>) and a key: <code>produtos/gatos/gato.jpg</code>.</li>
  <li>Everything goes through an <strong>HTTP API</strong>: PUT to write, GET to read, DELETE to remove. No disk to mount. B and the customer's browser read through the same URL.</li>
  <li>The slashes in the key look like folders, but they are just part of the name. The bucket is flat, and listing <code>produtos/gatos/</code> means filtering keys by prefix.</li>
  <li>The service <strong>replicates and distributes</strong> by itself, across disks and even across locations. The price is that you can't edit the middle of the object: fixing 4 KB of the header re-uploads the whole 2.4 MB.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li>It is the home for the store's photos, backups and large files: nearly unlimited, cheap capacity.</li>
  <li>It scales horizontally and is cloud native. In exchange, it doesn't fit a database or editing one byte in the middle of a file.</li>
  <li>Each access is a network call, with tens of milliseconds of latency, more than a disk. Put a CDN in front for photos.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={3}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Talking to S3 (examples)</h4>
  <pre><code>{"aws s3 cp gato.jpg s3://loja-fotos/produtos/gatos/gato.jpg\naws s3 ls s3://loja-fotos/produtos/gatos/\naws s3api head-object --bucket loja-fotos --key produtos/gatos/gato.jpg\n# head-object shows ContentLength, ContentType, ETag and metadata"}</code></pre>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Anatomy of an object</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Data:</strong> the photo's bytes.</li>
    <li><strong>Metadata:</strong> content type, date, tags you define.</li>
    <li><strong>Key:</strong> the unique identifier inside the bucket.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Durability</h4>
  <p>S3 Standard promises 99.999999999% (11 nines) durability: with 10 million objects, the expectation is to lose one every 10,000 years. That comes from keeping several copies in different locations.</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Other examples</h4>
  <p>Azure Blob Storage, Google Cloud Storage and, to run at home, MinIO and Ceph.</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Lifecycle</h4>
  <p>Automatic rules move old objects to cheaper tiers or delete them. Useful for the store's backups.</p>
