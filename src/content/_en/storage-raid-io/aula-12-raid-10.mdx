<p>pedidos.db is a write-heavy database, and RAID 5 and RAID 6 charge 4 and 6 operations per small write. RAID 10 combines what we've already seen: mirroring (RAID 1) to protect and stripes (RAID 0) for speed. It calculates no parity at all.</p>

<OneLiner>RAID 10 is mirroring with striping on top: first mirrored pairs, then data spread across the pairs, combining speed and protection at the cost of half the space.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>The store wants the best for pedidos.db: <strong>4 disks of 1 TB</strong>.</li>
  <li>They form <strong>2 mirrored pairs</strong> (RAID 1): D1+D2 and D3+D4. Each pair is a whole "disk".</li>
  <li>Blocks are striped (RAID 0) across the 2 pairs: A1 goes to pair 1 and is copied onto both of its disks, A2 goes to pair 2, also copied. Reads reach ~4x and writes ~2x.</li>
  <li>When D2 dies, D1 has A1 and A3, and the other pair doesn't even notice. The rebuild just <strong>copies D1</strong> to the new disk, with no calculation: it is fast and light.</li>
  <li>Capacity: half, <strong>2 TB</strong> usable (50%). You can lose <strong>one disk per pair</strong> and carry on. If you lose <strong>both disks of the same pair</strong>, you lose everything.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li>For write-heavy databases (finance, health, orders), it is the classic choice.</li>
  <li>The write penalty is just 2 and the rebuild is simple: copy the mirror, without reading the rest.</li>
  <li>It is expensive: half the space goes to redundancy. Until the rebuild finishes, D1 is the pair's only copy.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={3}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">The math</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Minimum:</strong> 4 disks, in an even number.</li>
    <li><strong>Usable capacity:</strong> N / 2 x size. 4 x 1 TB: 2 TB (50%).</li>
    <li><strong>Failures tolerated:</strong> 1 guaranteed, up to N / 2 if it is one per pair.</li>
    <li><strong>Write penalty:</strong> 2.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4 class="deep-h">What if two disks fail?</h4>
  <p>With 4 disks (D1, D2 mirrored; D3, D4 mirrored), there are 6 ways to pick 2 disks. In 4 of them the set survives (one from each pair). In 2 (D1 and D2, or D3 and D4) it dies. So there is a 2/3 chance of surviving the second failure.</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Random writes (150 IOPS per disk)</h4>
  <p>4 x 150 / 2 = 300 IOPS, double RAID 5 with the same 4 disks (4 x 150 / 4 = 150).</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">RAID 1+0 vs 0+1</h4>
  <p>In 1+0, you mirror first and then stripe: a failure affects only one pair. In 0+1 it is the reverse, and one failure takes down a whole side. That is why 1+0 is preferred.</p>
