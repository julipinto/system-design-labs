<p>There are five RAID levels now, and the store needs one for pedidos.db. The choice depends on what each piece of data can afford to lose: space, speed or safety. This lesson puts the five side by side.</p>

<OneLiner>each RAID trades space, speed and safety in its own way: 0 only runs fast, 1 and 10 copy, 5 and 6 use parity, and none of them is a backup.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Need speed and the data can be rebuilt?</strong> RAID 0.</li>
  <li><strong>Little data, critical, simple?</strong> RAID 1.</li>
  <li><strong>Write-heavy database with critical data?</strong> RAID 10. That is the case of pedidos.db.</li>
  <li><strong>Lots of space, read-heavy, tight budget?</strong> RAID 5. With large disks and many of them: RAID 6.</li>
  <li>In all of them: <strong>RAID is not a backup.</strong> Keep copies outside the set (object storage, another region).</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li>Capacity math with 1 TB disks: RAID 0 (4 disks) = 4 TB, RAID 1 (2) = 1 TB, RAID 5 (4) = 3 TB, RAID 6 (5) = 3 TB, RAID 10 (4) = 2 TB.</li>
  <li>RAID 5 and 6 pay for capacity with slower writes. RAID 10 pays with space. RAID 0 pays with safety.</li>
  <li>RAID protects against <strong>disk failure</strong>, not against human error, ransomware, fire or bugs.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={3}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">How to read the table</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>N</strong> is the number of disks and <strong>S</strong> the size of each one.</li>
    <li>Read and write are theoretical values: they depend on the controller, the cache and the access pattern.</li>
    <li><strong>Penalty</strong> is how many disk operations each small write generates.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Random writes with 150 IOPS per disk</h4>
  <ul>
    <li>RAID 10 with 4 disks: 4 x 150 / 2 = 300 IOPS.</li>
    <li>RAID 5 with 4 disks: 4 x 150 / 4 = 150 IOPS.</li>
    <li>RAID 6 with 5 disks: 5 x 150 / 6 = 125 IOPS.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4 class="deep-h">And the store?</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Orders (pedidos.db):</strong> RAID 10 on SSD, plus a replica on another machine.</li>
    <li><strong>Photos (like gato.jpg):</strong> object storage, which replicates by itself.</li>
    <li><strong>Backups:</strong> object storage in another region, with a lifecycle.</li>
    <li><strong>Cache and temporary files:</strong> RAID 0 or a disk with no RAID.</li>
  </ul>
