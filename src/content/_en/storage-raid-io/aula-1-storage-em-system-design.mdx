<p>An online store just opened. Every order, every product photo and every backup needs a place to live, and one disk can't serve all three.</p>

<OneLiner>storage is where the system keeps data so it survives a restart, and each kind of data asks for a different kind of disk.</OneLiner>

<DoIt>
  <p>Think about the <strong>online store</strong>. Write down 3 things it needs to keep (orders, photos, backups). For each one, answer: small or large? Mostly reads or mostly writes? Can it be lost?</p>
</DoIt>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>The store keeps <strong>orders</strong>: <strong>pedidos.db</strong>, with 200 GB of small rows, read and written all the time, at random spots.</li>
  <li>It keeps <strong>photos</strong>: 500,000 files like <strong>gato.jpg</strong> (2.4 MB), about 1.2 TB. Each photo is written once and read many times.</li>
  <li>It keeps <strong>backups</strong>: a copy of everything each night, written in sequence and almost never read.</li>
  <li>The three ask for different things: the database needs <strong>low latency</strong>, the photos need <strong>lots of cheap space</strong>, the backup needs <strong>durability</strong>.</li>
  <li>That is why storage comes in different shapes: <strong>DAS, NAS, block, file and object</strong>, plus the disk arrangements (<strong>RAID</strong>). The next lessons show one at a time.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li>A single disk is a <strong>single point of failure</strong>: if it dies, the store stops and the orders are gone. That is the pain RAID, at the end of the module, comes to solve.</li>
  <li>Picking the wrong storage is expensive: photos on NVMe is a waste, a database on a slow disk stalls checkout.</li>
  <li>Three numbers guide the choice: <strong>latency</strong>, <strong>IOPS and throughput</strong>, and <strong>cost per TB</strong>.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={3}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Typical latency by disk type</h4>
  <p>Order of magnitude for a random 4 KB read.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>HDD</strong> (disk with platters): 5 to 10 ms.</li>
    <li><strong>SATA SSD:</strong> about 0.2 ms.</li>
    <li><strong>NVMe:</strong> about 0.1 ms (100 microseconds).</li>
    <li><strong>Network:</strong> NAS and cloud disks add 0.2 to 0.5 ms of network on top, reaching about 1 ms per read.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4 class="deep-h">What "storage" covers</h4>
  <p>HDDs, SSDs and network solutions like NFS. Distributed systems also bring <strong>sharding</strong> (splitting the data across machines), <strong>replication</strong> (copying to other machines) and resilience, so you don't depend on a single host.</p>
