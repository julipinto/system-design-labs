<p>Last lesson the database asked for "low latency" and the photos asked for "lots of space". Words aren't enough to pick a disk. We need a calculation, and it has three numbers: IOPS, block and bandwidth.</p>

<OneLiner>IOPS counts how many requests the disk serves per second, bandwidth is the width of the pipe, and real throughput is IOPS times the block size, capped by the pipe.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>It all starts with an <strong>I/O</strong>: one read or write operation. <strong>IOPS</strong> is how many the disk does per second.</li>
  <li>Each operation has a size, the <strong>block</strong>. Postgres, for example, reads 8 KB pages.</li>
  <li>With IOPS and block size you get <strong>throughput</strong>: IOPS x block size. With 3,000 IOPS and 8 KB blocks: 3,000 x 8 KB = 24 MB/s.</li>
  <li>But the channel has a ceiling too, the <strong>bandwidth</strong>. AWS's default gp3 carries at most 125 MB/s.</li>
  <li>Both limits apply at the same time, so the <strong>lower one wins</strong>. Small blocks hit the IOPS limit. Large blocks hit the bandwidth limit.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li>Now we can translate the store's data. pedidos.db does lots of small operations: it needs <strong>IOPS</strong>. A product video is a large file: it needs <strong>bandwidth</strong>.</li>
  <li>Buying more IOPS when the bottleneck is bandwidth changes nothing, and the reverse is true too. Measure before you pay.</li>
  <li>Cloud applies <strong>throttling</strong>: the volume is "slow" because it hit the contracted limit, not because it is faulty. Monitor IOPS and MB/s.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={3}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Orders of magnitude (examples for study)</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>HDD 7200 rpm:</strong> ~150 random IOPS, ~180 MB/s sequential.</li>
    <li><strong>SATA SSD:</strong> tens of thousands of IOPS, interface ceiling at ~550 MB/s.</li>
    <li><strong>NVMe:</strong> hundreds of thousands of IOPS, several GB/s.</li>
    <li><strong>EBS gp3:</strong> 3,000 IOPS and 125 MB/s baseline. You can buy more of each, separately.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Random vs sequential</h4>
  <p>The HDD has to move the head for every random access: that is why it only gets ~150 IOPS. In sequence, the head stays still and the disk reads fast. An SSD barely feels the difference.</p>
  <h4 class="deep-h">See it on Linux</h4>
  <pre><code>{"iostat -x 1\n# columns: r/s and w/s (IOPS), rMB/s and wMB/s (throughput), r_await and w_await (latency)"}</code></pre>
  <h4 class="deep-h">The formula to remember</h4>
  <p>Real throughput = the lower of (IOPS x block) and (bandwidth). The block size where the two are equal is bandwidth / IOPS. On gp3: 125 MB/s divided by 3,000 gives about 42 KB.</p>
