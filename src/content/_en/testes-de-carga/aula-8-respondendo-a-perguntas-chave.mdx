<p>The article's journey (50% card, 30% instant payment, 15% boleto, 5% refund) becomes, in the store, <strong>card 60 · Pix 35 · refund 5</strong>. It is a declared example, with the Pix from module 12. At 200 orders/s, the generator sends 120 card, 70 Pix and 10 refunds per second.</p>

<h3 class="sec">Virtual users are not TPS</h3>

<p>Lesson 2 introduced the math: <strong>VUs = rate × (response time + pause)</strong>. For the plan's 200 orders/s, with 0.4 s of response and <strong>3 s of thinking</strong> (the customer filling in the address), that's (0.4 + 3) × 200 = <strong>680 VUs</strong>. In the open model, k6 fixes the rate and <strong>lets the VUs vary</strong>: <code>preAllocatedVUs</code> and <code>maxVUs</code> only reserve what the rate asks for.</p>

<Widget name="little" title="VUs, rate and pause" hint="Rate (1 to 400/s), response time and pause: computes the VUs (680 for 200/s) and converts the author's report's 800 users into a cycle (800 ÷ 600 = 1.33 s)." />

<h3 class="sec">The expensive endpoint changes the math</h3>

<p>Module 23 gave the cost: a page costs <strong>10 ms</strong> of CPU and a search <strong>80 ms</strong>. A mix with 5% searches has an average cost of 0.95 × 10 + 0.05 × 80 = <strong>13.5 ms</strong>, which reduces a catalog replica's capacity from 100 to <strong>74 req/s</strong>. For the plan's 950 req/s and ρ ≤ 70%, <strong>19</strong> replicas are needed, not 14.</p>

<Widget name="jornada" title="Journey and mix" hint="Checkout weights (card, Pix, refund) and catalog search, from 0% to 20%: shows the average cost per request, the capacity per replica and the replicas needed (14 with no search, 19 with 5%)." />

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: testing only the cheap endpoint</h3>

<p>A test that hits only <code>GET /produtos/42</code> shows the catalog handling <strong>100 req/s</strong> per replica. The real journey, with 5% searches, handles <strong>74</strong>. Whoever picks the cheap endpoint because it "represents the catalog" sizes <strong>14 replicas where 19 are needed</strong>: a five-replica gap that only shows up on Black Friday.</p>

<p>The same goes for the happy path: a test with only approved cards never sees the refund or the Pix. The article's key question is this one: <strong>what the customer does, how often, and what each action costs</strong>.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The question comes before the load.</strong> The nine questions define rate, mix, target and scenario; without them the test only injects load.</li>
  <li><strong>VUs come from the rate.</strong> 200 orders/s with 0.4 s and a 3 s pause is 680 VUs; the author's report doesn't give the pause.</li>
  <li><strong>The mix changes the capacity.</strong> 5% search drops the replica from 100 to 74 req/s: 14 replicas become 19.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
