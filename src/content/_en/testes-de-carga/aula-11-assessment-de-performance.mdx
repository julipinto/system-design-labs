<h3 class="sec">The conclusions and the backlog</h3>

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>"Today" breaks at 36 orders/s</strong> (target, confirmed in steady state: p95 of 798 ms) and ~42 (errors). The link is <code>pagamentos</code> with 2 pods. Above that the <em>goodput</em> drops from 40 to 26/s.</li>
  <li><strong>The plan breaks at ~250/s, not 300.</strong> <code>mkt-01</code>'s hot shard (60% of the traffic, 150/s ceiling). <strong>Action:</strong> the 2× dedicated shard (module 16).</li>
  <li><strong>With the dedicated shard the target falls at ~260/s</strong> in steady state (~270 on the ramp) and the errors at ~300/s. Module 23's sheet (sustainable <strong>210</strong> at 70%) is <strong>conservative by ~24%</strong> (260 ÷ 210), and the 200/s target has <strong>~30% headroom</strong> up to the breaking point (260 ÷ 200).</li>
  <li><strong>The test found nothing inside <code>pedidos</code>.</strong> It found what k6 can't see, in the next section.</li>
  <li><strong>Pending items.</strong> A real PSP (the simulator has <code>pagamentos</code> with exponential time, without the 8 s), cache, read replicas, elasticity (the HPA was <strong>off</strong>, as k6 says), the mix with searches and <code>entregas</code> with Kafka.</li>
</ol>

<p>Module 23's sheet is <strong>corrected</strong> like this: the sustainable 210 was the ρ ≤ 70% policy, which still holds as an operating rule. The test shows that the point where the target falls is ~260, and the distance between the two is the plan's margin.</p>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the assessment only sees what the test covered</h3>

<p><code>checkout.js</code> measures <strong>one flow</strong>. In the store, at 11 PM the <code>mkt-01</code> <strong>report</strong> runs, and the order status is queried all the time. Neither was in the script. The mixed scenario below (<code>pool.js</code>) puts together the checkout at 10/s, the order status at 40/s and the 11 PM report at 10 queries/s of 4 s, between 20 s and 50 s, against a server with a <strong>single pool of 20 connections</strong>. The comments in this file are in Portuguese, as in the lab's original.</p>

<pre><code>{poolJs}</code></pre>

<p>In the 25 to 50 s window, <strong>33% of the checkout orders</strong> (83 of 250) and <strong>10% of the status queries</strong> (101 of ~1,000) got a <code>503</code>, and the report was refused 53% of the time. Outside the window, 0%. Lena's order did nothing wrong: the report ate her pool.</p>

<p><code>checkout.js</code> would never see this, because it tests one flow at a time. Module 25 runs the same scenario with the pool split 16 + 4 and the customer sees 0% of <code>503</code>.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The report is the deliverable.</strong> Goals, scenarios, results and backlog: product and engineering read the same paper.</li>
  <li><strong>The numbers correct the sheet.</strong> The plan breaks at ~250/s without the dedicated shard, ~260/s with it; the sustainable 210 is conservative by ~24%.</li>
  <li><strong>The test only sees what the script covers.</strong> The mixed scenario found one flow knocking over another, and 1 in 3 checkout orders got a <code>503</code>.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={3}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">Corrections to the base article and the author's README</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>The lab's report doesn't add up.</strong> In stress, "2,000 users for 1 hour with an increase of 500 every 15 min": 4 steps of 500 go from 500 to 2,000 in 1 h, and the load doesn't stay at 2,000 for 1 h. In the spike, an average of 400 ms against "&lt; 400 ms" doesn't pass.</li>
    <li><strong>The README's numbers are examples.</strong> The repository has only the <code>README.md</code> and five images; <strong>there is no test script</strong>. This module's numbers were measured in the simulator and replace the ones there.</li>
    <li><strong>Tools.</strong> The article cites Locust, JMeter, Gatling and Oha; SDLabs uses k6. The article lacks the warning that the <strong>generator is also a system</strong> (here it shared the machine with the simulator), clock synchronization and distributed testing.</li>
    <li><strong>The simulator is a model:</strong> exponential per pod, round robin, no real PSP, Kafka, catalog or HPA.</li>
  </ul>

<p class="gancho">The test found the plan, the breaking point and <strong>one flow knocking over another by sharing the same pool</strong>. What's left is how to stop one flow, like the report or the <code>mkt-01</code> bot, from taking resources from the ones that aren't at fault: that's module 25, Bulkhead Pattern.</p>
