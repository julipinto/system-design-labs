<p class="ponte">In the last lesson the smoke proved the test runs, at 2 orders per second. Now the question is the <strong>normal store</strong>'s: at the load it expects, for long enough, does the target hold? Black Friday 2026 brought 30 orders/s to the checkout.</p>

<OneLiner><strong>Average load</strong> checks the target at the <strong>expected load</strong>, for <strong>long enough</strong> to show what only shows up with time. It answers "is the normal store doing fine?", not "does Black Friday fit?".</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Fixed rate.</strong> <code>TIPO=media RATE=30</code> uses <code>constant-arrival-rate</code>: 30 orders per second, with no ramp.</li>
  <li><strong>Time.</strong> 2 minutes here, to fit in an afternoon. The article says hours or days, to find memory leaks and patterns.</li>

<h3 class="sec">30 orders per second, 2 minutes</h3>

<p>In the "today" environment there were <strong>3,601</strong> requests with an <strong>average of 157.5 ms</strong>, median 129.5, p90 309.9, <strong>p95 381.3</strong>, <strong>p99 498.6</strong> and max 617.0 ms. Errors: <strong>0%</strong>. Both targets passed.</p>

<p>M/M/1 said ~259 ms on average, and k6 measured <strong>157 ms</strong>. The difference has a cause: the <code>lb-loja</code>'s <em>round robin</em> makes arrivals at each pod more regular than a random draw, and a queue with regular arrivals waits less. Module 23's model is <strong>conservative</strong>, and measuring tells you by how much.</p>

<h3 class="sec">The plan with the 2027 average</h3>

<p>The Black Friday 2027 average is <strong>125 orders/s</strong> (200 × 400 ÷ 640, the math from module 23). With <code>RATE=125</code> in the "plan" environment there were <strong>15,001</strong> requests, an <strong>average of 109.9 ms</strong>, <strong>p95 247.2</strong>, p99 357.4 and max 631.1 ms, with <strong>0%</strong> errors and exit code <code>0</code>. <code>shard-2</code> received 75/s of the 150 it can handle: <strong>50%</strong>.</p>

<p>The test passed with room to spare. Keep that phrase in mind, because the next section shows what it hides.</p>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the day's average is not the peak</h3>

<p>The 125/s passed with a p95 of 247 ms, and the breakpoint of the <strong>same environment</strong> breaks at ~250/s, <strong>double</strong> (lesson 7). If someone reads the average test as "the plan is validated", they conclude there are 125/s of headroom. The real headroom, at the 200/s peak, is much smaller.</p>

<p>The test wasn't wrong. It answered what was asked: is the normal store doing fine. The Black Friday question was a different one and calls for another type of test.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>Average load is the baseline.</strong> If the store doesn't pass at the expected load, the other tests make no sense.</li>
  <li><strong>Time reveals what rate doesn't.</strong> Memory leaks and patterns only show up in hours; that's why the article says to run it for a long time.</li>
  <li><strong>Passing with room to spare doesn't validate the peak.</strong> 125/s passes at a 247 ms p95 and the plan breaks at 250/s.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
