<p class="ponte">In the last lesson the smoke ran and both targets passed, at 2 orders per second. That doesn't say whether the store handles the plan's 200/s. Two similar questions need to be told apart: <strong>does the store do well at the load it expects?</strong> and <strong>what happens when more comes?</strong></p>

<OneLiner><strong>Load</strong> is the <strong>expected</strong>, measured against a target. <strong>Stress</strong> is the <strong>adverse</strong>: above it, in peaks, until you see what degrades. And <em>how</em> the load is injected (open or closed model) can hide the break.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Load:</strong> the <strong>expected</strong> behavior. The question is "does it hold the target?": 200 orders/s with p95 ≤ 800 ms.</li>
  <li><strong>Stress:</strong> the <strong>adverse</strong> behavior. The question is "what degrades first and how does it come back?": 300/s, peaks and bursts.</li>
  <li>The <strong>five types</strong> (smoke, average load, stress, spike and breakpoint) fit on both sides. k6 also names the <em>soak</em>, average load for hours.</li>
  <li><strong>Open model:</strong> the generator fixes the <strong>rate</strong> (<code>*-arrival-rate</code>) and lets the VUs vary. 54 orders/s arrive, whether the system is answering or not.</li>
  <li><strong>Closed model:</strong> the generator fixes the <strong>VUs</strong> (<code>constant-vus</code>). Each VU only sends the next order when the previous one comes back, so the rate depends on the system.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">Users are not orders per second</h3>

<p>The author's report talks about "500 simultaneous users" and "600 TPS with 800 active users". Users and rate are different units, and the bridge between them is Little's Law: <strong>VUs = rate × (response time + pause)</strong>. With 800 users and 600 TPS, each user's cycle is 800 ÷ 600 = <strong>1.33 s</strong>. Without the declared pause, the numbers don't convert. Lesson 8 does this math for the store's 200 orders/s.</p>

<h3 class="sec">The same system, two generators</h3>

<p>The "today" environment has a capacity of ~40 orders/s, capped by <code>pagamentos</code> (2 pods × 20/s). Rounds of 60 s each:</p>

<p>With 20 VUs Little's Law checks out: 20 = 38.07 × 0.520. Throughput doesn't go past ~40/s no matter how many VUs you add, because each VU <strong>waits</strong> for the response before sending another. The closed generator regulates itself and never shows the 52% error that the open one showed at 54/s, which is a rate the store can really receive.</p>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the closed test passes</h3>

<p>A test with 20 VUs and no pause ends with <strong>0% errors</strong> and a p95 of 1.07 s. The p95 is already past the 800 ms target (k6 exits with <code>99</code>), but no errors show up, and anyone who reads only the errors says everything is fine. The closed generator <strong>hides the break</strong> because when the system slows down, it slows down too. This is what the k6 documentation calls <em>coordinated omission</em>: the generator omits the requests it should have sent while it was waiting.</p>

<p>The store's customers don't wait: the 8 PM newsletter arrives at a rate, not at a number of users who answer one at a time. For that case, the model is the open one.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>Load and stress ask different things.</strong> One has a pass target; the other looks for what degrades and how it comes back.</li>
  <li><strong>"Users" don't convert to rate without the pause.</strong> 800 ÷ 600 = 1.33 s of cycle; without that, the number is just a guess.</li>
  <li><strong>The closed generator hides the break.</strong> 20 VUs gave 0% errors where a rate of 54/s gave 52%.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

  <h4 class="deep-h">Corrections to the base article</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>"Simultaneous users" as a unit of load.</strong> The article's and the lab's report use 500, 1,300 and 3,700 users and "600 TPS with 800 active users". Users are the <strong>closed</strong> model; rate is the open model's <strong>input</strong>. Here, 20 VUs gave 38.1/s and 0% errors, against 52% at 54/s in the open model.</li>
    <li><strong>"Stress is a much higher load".</strong> What stress measures is how the system degrades and recovers (lesson 5), not only the size of the load.</li>
  </ul>
