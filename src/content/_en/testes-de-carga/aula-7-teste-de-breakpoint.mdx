<p class="ponte">In the last lesson the 60 orders per second burst broke "today", but didn't say <strong>at how much</strong> it breaks. The breakpoint answers that: it raises the load slowly until it breaks and marks the point.</p>

<OneLiner>The <strong>breakpoint</strong> raises the load <strong>until it breaks</strong> and says <strong>where</strong>. "Breaking" has <strong>three points</strong>: the <strong>target</strong> (p95 &gt; 800 ms), the <strong>errors</strong> (&gt; 0.1%) and the <strong>collapse</strong> (the <em>goodput</em> drops).</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Ramp.</strong> <code>ramping-arrival-rate</code> from 1 to <code>TOPO</code> orders/s over <code>DUR</code>: <code>TIPO=breakpoint TOPO=60 DUR=3m ABORT=0 k6 run checkout.js</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>10 s windows.</strong> The CSV (<code>--out csv=bp.csv</code>) feeds <code>analisa.py</code>, which computes p50, p95, p99, errors and completed per window.</li>
  <li><strong>Offered rate.</strong> It is the ramp's rate in the middle of the window; <em>completed/s</em> is what came back.</li>
  <li><strong>Three points.</strong> Target, errors and collapse, read from the windows.</li>
  <li><strong>Confirm by step.</strong> A constant rate for 2 min confirms the point that the ramp only located.</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<p>Total: 5,470 requests, <strong>18.84%</strong> failures, <code>dropped_iterations</code> 19. The three points: the <strong>target</strong> falls between <strong>35 and 39</strong> orders/s offered (two rounds: p95 of 820 ms in the 35.4/s window and 932 ms in the 38.7/s one, because the break varies between rounds); the <strong>errors</strong> start at <strong>~42/s</strong>, which is 2 pods × 20/s of <code>pagamentos</code>; and the <strong>collapse</strong> comes later, when the <em>goodput</em> drops from ~40 to ~26/s. Above the ceiling, <strong>more load means fewer completed orders</strong>.</p>

<h3 class="sec">The plan and plan 2</h3>

<p>On the "plan" (3 shards, <code>mkt-01</code> at 60%, ramp from 1 to 360/s in 4 min) the p95 stays at 196–284 ms up to ~160/s and then climbs: 388 ms at 188/s, 444 at 203, 491 at 233, 763 at 248 and <strong>928 ms at 263/s</strong>. The target crosses at <strong>~250/s</strong>. Errors start at 278/s (p95 of 1,576 ms and 0.3%) and reach 4.8% at 293/s, 11.4% at 308/s and 40.9% at 353/s. The maximum <em>goodput</em> was ~271/s and dropped to 195/s with 353 offered. Total: 42,726 requests, 7.69% failures, <code>dropped_iterations</code> 593.</p>

<p>The plan's breaking point is <strong>~250/s, not 300/s</strong>, and the cause is the <strong>hot shard</strong>: 60% of 250 is <strong>150/s</strong>, the shard's ceiling from module 16. The math "55.6% efficiency = 250/s useful" was confirmed by k6 within 3%.</p>

<p>On "plan 2" (<code>mkt-01</code> on a 2× dedicated shard, same ramp) the p95 goes from 338 ms at 188/s to 421 at 218, 583 at 233, 728 at 263 and <strong>1,003 ms at 278/s</strong>: the target crosses at <strong>~270/s</strong> (90% of nominal). Errors start at ~300/s: 0.6% at 308/s, 3.1% at 323, 10.6% at 338 and 32.2% at 353. The maximum <em>goodput</em> was ~292/s. At ~300/s the next link is <strong>10 pods × 30/s</strong> (<code>pedidos</code>) and <strong>15 × 20/s</strong> (<code>pagamentos</code>) <strong>together</strong>, and the p95 falls earlier, at 90% of nominal.</p>

<h3 class="sec">k6 cuts on the cumulative</h3>

<p>With <code>ABORT=1</code>, <code>abortOnFail</code> stopped the test at <strong>128 s</strong> (≈ 43 orders/s offered) with a <strong>cumulative</strong> <code>p(95)=894 ms</code> (2,764 requests) and no errors. <strong>By window</strong>, the p95 had already crossed 800 ms in the 100–110 s window (820 ms, ≈ 35 orders/s). The automatic cut came late: ~8 orders/s and ~20 s more. To locate the point, use the <strong>window</strong> (the CSV or Prometheus), not the aggregate.</p>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the generator becomes the bottleneck, or the environment is elastic</h3>

<p>First case: the <strong>generator</strong>. In the plan's round <code>dropped_iterations</code> was <strong>593</strong> (1.4%): k6 fell behind and stopped sending orders it should have sent. k6 and the server shared the same machine, so the generator competed with the system. Monitor the generator's CPU and check whether <code>vus</code> gets close to <code>maxVUs</code>.</p>

<p>Second case: <strong>a breakpoint in an elastic environment</strong>. With the HPA on, the system adds replicas while the load climbs and the test "finds the limit of the cloud bill". k6 says to <strong>turn elasticity off</strong> to find the <strong>system's</strong> limit, run in <em>staging</em> and test autoscaling in a separate test.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>"Breaking" has three points.</strong> Target (35–39/s, today), errors (~42/s) and collapse (goodput at ~26/s): each calls for a different action.</li>
  <li><strong>The plan breaks at ~250/s, not 300.</strong> <code>mkt-01</code>'s hot shard (60% × 250 = 150/s) is the link; the dedicated shard takes it to ~260/s.</li>
  <li><strong>Use the window, not the cumulative.</strong> k6's cut at 128 s came ~20 s and ~8 orders/s after the real point.</li>
</WhyItMatters>
