<p class="ponte">In the last lesson choreography left 1057 without an owner: the event gets lost and nobody notices, and the flow isn't written anywhere. The other answer is to put the whole map <strong>in one place</strong>. That place is the <strong>orchestrator</strong>: it gives the orders, waits for the reply and decides when to compensate.</p>

<OneLiner>In <strong>orchestration</strong>, an <strong>orchestrator</strong> holds the saga's map, sends <strong>commands</strong> to the services and waits for <strong>replies</strong>; it is the one who orders the compensation. The flow lives <strong>in one place</strong> and the services don't know each other, but the orchestrator is an extra component that <strong>every order goes through</strong>.</OneLiner>

<HowItWorks>
<ol class="flow steps">
  <li><strong>Command and reply.</strong> Each service has a <strong>command</strong> topic and a <strong>reply</strong> topic. In the author's lab they are <code>pagamentos-commands</code> and <code>pagamentos-reply</code>, and the same for <code>pedidos</code>, <code>estoque</code> and <code>entregas</code>. The orchestrator writes to the first and reads from the second.</li>
  <li><strong>1057 step by step.</strong> The orchestrator sends <code>CobrarPedido</code> and gets <code>PedidoPago</code>. It sends <code>ReservarEstoque</code> and gets the failure. Then it sends <code>EstornarCobranca</code>, gets the ok, sends <code>CancelarPedido</code> and saves <code>CANCELADO</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>How many messages.</strong> On success there are <strong>6</strong> (3 commands and 3 replies); on failure, <strong>8</strong> (charge and reply, reserve and failure reply, refund and reply, cancel and reply), plus <strong>1</strong> to start. Choreography used 4 and 5 events: the orchestrator costs more messages.</li>
  <li><strong>What you gain.</strong> The flow is <strong>in one place</strong>: metrics, a watchdog and pausing come easy. The services <strong>don't know each other</strong>: <code>estoque</code> only understands the <code>ReservarEstoque</code> command.</li>
  <li><strong>What you pay.</strong> A component that <strong>every</strong> order goes through (the blast radius from module 21) and the temptation to put <strong>business rules</strong> in it, until it becomes the store's "god service".</li>
</ol>
</HowItWorks>

<h3 class="sec">What the author's lab does</h3>

<p>The author's lab (public repository, only <code>orquestrado/</code> exists) has the orchestrator in <code>orquestrador/main.go</code>. This excerpt is the core: the reply arrives on a topic and the orchestrator sends the next command (read, <strong>not run</strong>; unchanged, with the original's line numbers; the comments were translated to English).</p>

<CodeLab code={orquestradorMain} from={327} to={364} />

<ol class="flow steps">
  <li>Line 334 picks the next step by the <strong>topic</strong> that replied: <code>pedidos-reply</code> sends <code>RESERVE_STOCK</code> (lines 335 to 348).</li>
  <li><code>estoque-reply</code> sends <code>PROCESS_PAYMENT</code> and <code>pagamentos-reply</code> sends <code>SCHEDULE_DELIVERY</code>. That is the saga's map, written as four <code>case</code>s.</li>
  <li>Notice what is <strong>not</strong> here: no check of the current state before advancing. Lesson 7 shows what that causes.</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="sec">What if it goes wrong: the orchestrator goes down with 200 sagas in flight</h3>

<p>The orchestrator goes down with <strong>200</strong> sagas in progress (example; at the Black Friday peak, with Pix waiting 41.2 s, the number of orders waiting goes past a thousand). If their state lived in its <strong>memory</strong>, all of them are lost: the services did the steps, and nobody knows which step each order is at. That's why the state lives in a <strong>database</strong>: when the orchestrator comes back, it reads the table, sees 1057 in <code>COBRANDO</code> and carries on from where it stopped. Lesson 6 shows what structure that is.</p>

<WhyItMatters>
  <li><strong>The map has an address.</strong> To know where 1057 is, you ask the orchestrator, not four services.</li>
  <li><strong>The services don't know each other.</strong> Swapping <code>estoque</code> doesn't touch <code>pagamentos</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>It costs more messages and one more component.</strong> 6 and 8 against 4 and 5, and a point every order goes through.</li>
  <li><strong>State outside memory.</strong> An orchestrator that keeps everything in memory loses the sagas when it goes down.</li>
</WhyItMatters>

<Deep min={5}>
  <h4 class="deep-h">A synchronous orchestrator exists too</h4>
  <p>The orchestrator can call the services by request and response, instead of topics. It's simpler to follow, but <em>retry</em>, <em>backoff</em> and the dead-letter queue (module 13) become <strong>its</strong> job: if <code>estoque</code> doesn't answer, the orchestrator decides what to do.</p>
